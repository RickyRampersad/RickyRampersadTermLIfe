Group Client Training

Training and test

The staff manual for group client management, then the test. Sign in with your agent number and password from the Agent Skill Bank, so your reading and your result are logged against your name.

1The job, and why it matters

Each of your groups is an employer that pays for its employees’ life, health and pension plans with Guardian Life. To that employer, you are Guardian Life.

Every Monday at 10:00 each group gets one letter from you: what we completed, what is still open, what we need from them, and a link to their own service page. You check the group and approve its letter during the week; at ten on Monday it goes out by itself from support@. On that page they read each item, mark it Correct, Needs a change or Not ours, and rate our service.

So the letter is a promise that every item on it is true today. A wrong item, an item the group already answered, a date we let slip without a word: each costs more trust than a late letter ever would.

  • Your groups are the accounts you own in Salesforce. A group moves to someone else when the account owner is changed in Salesforce.
  • Three places, three jobs. Salesforce is the record. The inboxes are the truth of what was said. The group page (rickyrampersadbranch.com/groupclientmanagement) is what the group will read.

2Your week

Monday10:00: the letters go by themselves. Every letter you approved is written again from Salesforce and sent. One that waited (a new item since you checked, a reason missing) comes to you by e-mail: put it right and approve it again, and it goes in the next hourly run. Then follow up any group that did not answer last week (section 7).
Tue – WedWork the open items. Keep Salesforce current as you go: status, target dates, Chatter, and e-mails filed on the record.
ThursdayGroups answer by Friday; each answer reaches you by e-mail, and you act on it by the next working day (section 7). Start the six checks (section 3) on each of your groups.
FridayApprove. Finish the checks, deal with every escalation (section 4), write the reasons (section 6), then preview and approve each group marked Approve for Monday. You can approve up to 10:00 on Monday, but Friday leaves Monday morning for what changed.
Every dayRead support@ and your own inbox. Nothing from a group waits more than a working day for at least an acknowledgement.

3Before you approve: the six checks

The letter is written again from Salesforce at ten on Monday, and an item that appears after you approve holds it. These checks make Salesforce true first.

  1. Open the group and read it. Read the chips: Approve for Monday, waiting on them, past target, needs a reason. Read Letter goes to: is it the right person? (Personal mailboxes: section 5.)
  2. Check every open Salesforce task. Is it still open? Is the status right? Is the target date real? Is the right person on it? Close what is done. If a date has passed and you cannot finish today, set a realistic new date with a Chatter note saying why. Never move a date just to clear the red: every move is recorded, and the group sees the first target date beside the current one.
  3. Scrub the inboxes. Search support@, your own Outlook and the sales support inbox for the group’s name, its list bill, the contact’s e-mail address and each item’s subject line. Look for: an e-mail from the group nobody answered (answer it first); a document or form they sent (update the item); a payment confirmation (check the billing and close the item if it is settled); a complaint (section 4). Not every e-mail is filed in Salesforce, so the inbox is the truth. File what matters on the record, so the item’s history shows it.
  4. See what the group actually has. In Salesforce, open the account: its policies and list bill, the latest billing record (amount, date, status), and who is joining or leaving. Compare that with what the open items say. A letter that contradicts the group’s own billing or headcount tells them we do not know their business.
  5. Update, then refresh. Salesforce first: statuses, dates, Chatter notes, e-mails filed. Then tap Refresh from Salesforce on the group page, so the board and the letter read the new state.
  6. Preview, check, approve. Write the reason for any item open more than 30 days (section 6). Preview: the right contact and greeting, the group’s items ticked and nothing else, your opening line if you want one. Tick I have checked every item against Salesforce and the latest billing, then Approve for Monday. If something changes before Monday, approve it again. Who approved it, the letter, its items and when it went are all logged.
Untick an item only when it is not the group’s to see (section 5). Never untick to hide a problem. If it is true and it is theirs, they see it.

Escalating is not passing it on. You still own the item: you tell the manager what is blocking it and what you have already done.

5What the group sees, and what it never sees

  • Shown unless you untick: items on the group’s own account, its billing records, or naming the group.
  • Hidden unless you tick: items reached only through an employee’s own policy or contact. Tick one only when it is plan administration the employer handles, such as someone joining or leaving the plan. A member’s personal matters (their beneficiary, a loan, a change to their own policy) stay hidden.
  • Never, whatever is ticked: a member’s health or a claim. The system keeps those items with staff. Never work around it by e-mail, by phone or in the letter. If HR asks, explain that a member’s health matters are between the member and Guardian Life, and that we deal with the member directly.
  • Chatter is where staff write to each other. A group sees a comment only when you tick it, and only a note written for the group should ever be ticked. A note you write for the group on the page is posted to Chatter and shown to them at once.
  • The access code. The letter carries the group’s list bill and access code, and anyone holding them can read the group’s page. So the letter goes only to the group’s contact: the Contact Person on the account in Salesforce. If that is a personal mailbox (gmail, hotmail, yahoo), confirm it is still their contact before you send; if it is not, update the Contact Person in Salesforce first. Never forward the letter, and never send it to another employee to pass on.

6Writing to the group

The note under an item is plain facts, a date that is in Salesforce, and what happens next, in one or two sentences.

Good “Your September leavers went for processing on 1 October. We expect the corrected bill by 16 October.”
Never blame (“head office has been slow again”), internal names or departments, words copied from Chatter, a date you cannot keep, or anything about a member’s health.
  • Items open more than 30 days need a note shared in the last 30 days before the letter can go. The page marks each one.
  • Draft with AI writes a first draft from the item’s history. Read it, correct anything that does not match Salesforce, then Post. Nothing is shared until you press Post, and once you do, it is yours.
  • Your opening line is optional: a thank-you, or a heads-up about something important this week. It is never the place for internal problems.
  • On the phone the same rules hold: own it, give plain facts and a date, then do what you said.

7After Monday’s letter: follow-up

  • Answers come in by Friday. Each one reaches you by e-mail, and any note the group writes goes on the task’s Chatter.
  • Act by the next working day. Needs a change: fix it or find out why, tell the group what you are doing, note it in Chatter. Not ours: find out whose it is, correct Salesforce, tell the group. A rating of 3 or less: call the contact within two working days, and tell the branch manager.
  • No answer by Friday? Before you approve the next letter, call or e-mail the contact, check they got the letter and can sign in, and note it in Chatter. Never answer for the group: its answers are its own.
  • Keep your promises. When you give a group a date, put it in Salesforce. If it is going to slip, tell the group before the date, not after, with the new date and what you are doing. Then confirm when it is done.
  • Close the loop. An item is done when the group’s problem is solved and they know it, not when the task is closed.

8Taking initiative

Initiative is fixing the cause, not just the item, and doing it before anyone asks.

  • See the pattern. The same item stuck on three groups is one problem. Raise it once with the details, copy the branch manager, set a follow-up date, and tell each group what you are doing.
  • Don’t wait for a colleague. If a group is waiting on an item that sits with someone on leave, take it over with a Chatter note to your colleague, and tell the group.
  • Fix records at the source. A contact who left, a wrong list bill, a missing e-mail: correct it in Salesforce, not around it. When a letter bounces, call the group, get the new contact, update the Contact Person, and approve the letter again.
  • Ask before the deadline. If head office needs something from the group, ask early, not on the day it is due.
  • Bring a next step. When you cannot solve it today, tell the group what happens next and when.
  • Tell the manager what you find: a gap in a process, a group at risk, a better way of doing it. The board shows the work; you see the reasons.

9How you are appraised, and the test

The test is weighted like your appraisal. Each competency’s weight is its share of the marks:

  • To pass: 80% overall, every critical question right, and no competency under 60%. The critical questions are on confidentiality.
  • Each competency is rated Exceeds (90% and over), Meets (75–89%), Developing (60–74%) or Not yet (under 60%).
  • Written answers are marked by AI against a marking guide, and your manager confirms the mark. Write them the way you would do the work: the steps, in order, and why.
  • Your result is logged with feedback on every question, and e-mailed to you and the branch manager. You may take the test again; every attempt is kept.
  • On the job the same competencies show on the board: letters approved for Monday, items past target, how many items groups mark Correct, ratings, and how quickly answers are followed up.
