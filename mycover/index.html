Private Motor · Comprehensive

You are covered for more than you think — and less than you think, in one place that matters.

Comprehensive means Sections 1, 2, 3 and 4 of your policy are live. Section 2 pays for your own car. Section 3 pays for what you do to other people. Section 4 is the part almost nobody reads — and it is already paid for.

Already yours, at no extra cost

Accident cover for you and your husband or wife up to 100%

Death, or losing sight or a hand or foot, pays the full limit of liability — and it follows you into any private car, not only your own. If you cannot work at all, it pays $50 a week for up to 26 weeks. Applies between 17 and 65.

Medical expenses included

You, your driver, and anyone riding with you. Reasonable medical expenses for each person hurt in the accident.

Your garage, if it burns included

If your private garage is damaged by fire and no other policy covers it, this one does.

Luggage and personal effects included

Things in the car, lost by accident, fire or theft. Not money, jewellery, cameras, laptops or cell phones — those are exactly what people assume are covered.

Towing to safety included

If the car cannot be driven after a covered accident, we pay to move it to the nearest repairer or a place of safety.

Authorise your own repair up to the limit

You do not have to wait for us to see it first. Send the estimate, the accident report form and a photograph, and get on with your life.

What is not covered — read this one twice

Flood, hurricane, windstorm, tornado and earthquake are excluded from every motor policy as standard. In Trinidad and Tobago. That is General Exclusion 2(a), and it applies whether you are comprehensive or not.

The fix is one line on your schedule: Optional Extra Benefit 1, "Special Perils", which deletes that exclusion entirely. If OEB 1 is not printed on your schedule, a flooded car is your own bill. Look now — we will wait.

Windscreen glass excluded

A cracked windscreen on its own is not covered unless OEB 2 is on your schedule. With it, there is no excess and it does not touch your No Claim Discount.

Tyres on their own excluded

Damage to tyres is only covered if the car is damaged at the same time.

Wear, tear and breakdown excluded

Mechanical or electrical failure is not an accident. But if the failure causes a collision, the collision damage is covered.

Market value, not what you paid the ceiling

A total loss pays the market value or your sum insured — whichever is lower. Insuring for more than the car is worth buys you nothing. This is why the value comes down each year.

What you can add

OEB 1 · Special Perils the big one

Deletes the flood, hurricane, windstorm and earthquake exclusion. In this country this is not an optional extra so much as the point.

OEB 2 · Windscreen & all glass no excess

Glass-only damage, with no excess and no effect on your NCD. Replace it and your cover reinstates free once a year up to $2,500.

OEB 5 · Agreed Value 15% a year

Pays your sum insured on a total loss instead of market value, less 15% a year pro-rata. It only works if you give us a written valuation from an approved valuator at each renewal — without one it quietly reverts to market value.

OEB 6 · Hospitalisation $15,000 / year

Surgery, ICU, room and board, ambulance. Halves at 65 and stops at 70. Pre-existing conditions from the two years before you started are excluded.

OEB 4 · Courtesy car $100 / day

A replacement car, or $100 a day toward a rental, from the day we accept the claim until the repair is finished.

OEB 3 · Passengers per seat

The same accident benefit you and your spouse have, extended to whoever is in the car. Worth a conversation if you carry other people's children.

HomeGuard · Buildings and Contents

Your house is covered for the weather. Your car is not.

This surprises almost everyone. HomeGuard covers flood, earthquake, hurricane, tsunami and landslip as standard causes of loss — they are causes 2, 3, 4 and 6 on your policy. The motor policy excludes those same events unless you buy them back.

The limits inside contents

Unless an item is listed by name on your schedule, contents cover pays at most:

· 2% of the contents sum insured for any one valuable · 10% for all valuables in a single event · 5% for any one other item, or any one piece of electronic equipment.

On TT$200,000 of contents that makes a TT$45,000 ring worth TT$4,000 at claim time. Listing it costs very little. Not listing it costs the difference.

Empty for 30 days cover stops

If the house is unoccupied 30 consecutive days, theft, malicious damage and escape of water fall away. Tell us before you travel — it is usually a simple endorsement.

Not maintained reduced

If the building has not been kept in good repair, a deduction for wear and tear comes off the settlement.

Specified Personal Items all risks

Sub-section E covers named items against almost anything, anywhere in the territorial limits — the answer to the 2% problem above.
