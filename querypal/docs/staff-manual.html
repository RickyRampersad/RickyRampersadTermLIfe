01 Signing in

Your code is your identity

Open querymypolicies.netlify.app and choose Agent. Sign in with your agent number and, where one has been set for you, your password.

What you seeWhat it meansWhat to do
Signed in, your name shownAll good.Carry on.
Not on the branch listThe number was not recognised. Check you typed your number, not your name or email. Case and spaces don't matter.
Password doesn't matchYour row has a password set. Use it. If you don't know it, ask the branch — it can be reset in seconds.
Too many attemptsGuess-protection kicked in. Wait ten minutes, then try once, carefully.
House rule

Your code is yours. It decides whose cases you can see and whose name goes on the work. Never sign in with someone else's number, and never share yours — including with a client.

02 Logging a request

Five screens, about a minute
  1. Who is it forClient, agent, or company. Pick Agent when you are logging on a client's behalf — your name goes on the case and the credit is yours.
  2. What do they needChoose in plain language. You never need to know a department or an email address — the system already knows where every request type goes.
  3. Check the promiseThe card shows the department and the turnaround, and names the date the answer is due. Say that date to the client out loud. It is the promise.
  4. Fill in the detailsA checklist shows exactly what to have ready, so the department doesn't come back asking. Attach documents right on the form — PDF, Word, Excel, CSV or photos, up to five files.
  5. SubmitYou get a reference. Give it to the client, or send them the PDF receipt.
Do this

Write the description as if the officer reading it has never met this client. One clear sentence about what is wanted saves two days of back-and-forth.

03 What happens the moment you submit

Four things, instantly
  • The department receives a branded email with the full request and any documents.
  • You, your manager and the branch are copied, so nobody is working blind.
  • The case is logged with a reference in the form RRB/2026/214/Client Name/Subject and a working-day deadline.
  • A Salesforce case is created, so the record exists where the company expects it.

From that moment the case is on the branch board, and the clock is running.

04 The chasing, done for you

You do not follow up. The system does.

Once a day the autopilot sweeps every open case. If a deadline has passed with no reply, it chases the department on the same email thread, so the whole history stays in one place.

Meanwhile the client is not left guessing:

  • Every few working days they get a progress note — "actively being worked on" — with a live progress gauge. They are told they need do nothing.
  • If a case is put on hold, they are told what that means and that nothing is lost.
  • When it closes, they get a one-tap star rating email. Their score lands on your record.
Health and claims

Health and claims items route to Sales Support and are chased daily until closed, with the Claims & Maturity portal block (10:00–12:00) named in every reminder.

05 Your dashboard

Your book, and what the system did for you

Signed in, you see every case in your name: status, the promised turnaround, days elapsed, how many chases went out on your behalf, and the client's rating once it arrives.

  • Agents see their own cases.
  • Managers see their whole unit.
  • Branch sees everything.
  • Staff see the cases assigned to them for action.

You can add a comment to any open case, chase now if a client is waiting on the phone, and close a case in the app when the answer comes back.

06 Clients and companies

They can serve themselves

Clients and corporate clients have their own portal. They sign in with an access code — and if they don't have one, they request it on the page and it arrives by email automatically, only ever to the address already on our records.

Once in, a company can:

  • see every employee case with a live progress gauge and their own service statistics — response rate, resolution rate, average days;
  • read the full history of a case and add comments, which land on the case record;
  • enroll a new member with the forms attached — it goes straight to Group Insurance Administration and is chased like anything else;
  • report a leaver, which ends cover and sends the 30-day conversion notice.
Never

Internal branch notes are filtered out of everything a client can see — but that only works if notes are entered as internal. Never type an internal remark into a client-facing comment.

07 Closing a case

Close it the moment it's answered

When the department replies with the answer, the system usually notices and closes the case itself. If it doesn't, close it in the app. Closing does three things: it stops the chasers, it fills in the date closed and days to close, and it triggers the client's rating email.

Do not

Do not leave an answered case open. It keeps chasing a department that has already replied, it drags down the branch's on-time figure, and it costs you your own on-time record.

08 The branch wall

How we're doing, in the open

Any screen at querymypolicies.netlify.app/wall shows the live branch board — no sign-in needed. It rotates through eight boards every fifteen seconds and refreshes every minute: the service pulse, twelve weeks of demand, open work against deadlines, department performance, the agent leaderboard, what clients ask for most, satisfaction, and a scorecard of how we're doing against the promises we make.

Tap Sound once and the wall plays a quiet score and reads the headline out loud.

Worth knowing

The wall shows totals only — never a client name, policy number or reference — so it is safe on any screen the public can see. Your name appears on the leaderboard when you are resolving on time.

09 Rules that matter

Six things to get right
  1. Log it in Query Pal, not by email. A request sent straight to a department has no reference, no deadline, no chasing, and no record that you did the work.
  2. Give the client the reference and the date. That is the promise we are measured on.
  3. Attach at the form. Documents sent separately get separated.
  4. Never put a password, full card number or PIN in a description. The description travels to the department and into the record.
  5. Keep internal notes internal. Clients can read the case trail.
  6. Close what is finished. See section 07.
