The eight openings

What changes, and who gets which. Each one leads with the thing the client is glad to hear.

AA policy has maturedgoes to a client with a policy that has matured
A policy of yours has matured — there is money to claim
What they are glad to hear: money is waiting to be claimed.

One of your policies reached the end of its term and has matured. That money is yours, and it is sitting there waiting to be claimed. Nothing about this is a sales call — we would simply rather you had it than not.

Your representative, {{agent_first_name}}, has moved on from Guardian Life, which is why this is coming from the branch. Your other policies are completely unaffected.

BPaid upgoes to a client whose policy is paid up
You have finished paying for this one
What they are glad to hear: they own it outright and may not know what it is worth.

Your policy is paid up. There is nothing more to pay, and the cover stands for the rest of your life. Most people in that position have never been told what it is now worth, or what it can still do for them.

Your representative, {{agent_first_name}}, has moved on from Guardian Life. Your policy is completely unaffected — it is yours, outright.

CCarrying waiver of premiumgoes to a client carrying a waiver of premium
There is a benefit inside your policy you may not know about
What they are glad to hear: a benefit inside the policy they were probably never told about.

In plain terms: if illness or injury stopped you working, Guardian Life would keep paying your premiums for you, and your cover would stay in force while you recovered. You have been paying for that protection all along. Most people who hold it have never been told.

Your representative, {{agent_first_name}}, has moved on from Guardian Life. Your policy, and that benefit inside it, are completely unaffected.

DCollected off a payrollgoes to a client whose premium comes off a payroll
Your policy, and a check we are running on your salary deduction
What they are glad to hear: we are checking the deduction so they do not have to.

Your premium comes off your salary. Those arrangements sometimes stop quietly — a change of employer, a payroll system, a posting — and the first anybody notices is a gap. We are checking your deduction against our own records this week, so that if something has slipped we find it rather than you. You do not need to do anything. And if you would ever like to know exactly what Guardian Life has recorded as paid on your policy, ask the branch for your paid-to date — it is on your file and takes one call.

Your representative, {{agent_first_name}}, has moved on from Guardian Life. Your policy is completely unaffected.

EHeld ten years or moregoes to a client who has held cover for ten years or more
You have held this cover for {{years}} years
What they are glad to hear: a price nobody can sell them again.

Your premium is fixed on the age you were when you took this policy out — not your age now. A policy written for you {{years}} years ago is still charging {{years}}-years-ago prices, and no company can sell that price back to you. It is the part of a life policy people give away without realising they owned it. The value inside it is not sitting idle either: after three years of premiums, the Insurance Act entitles you to a smaller policy fully paid for life instead of cashing it in, and the branch will put that figure in writing beside what a surrender would pay before you decide anything.

Your representative, {{agent_first_name}}, has moved on from Guardian Life. Your policy, and that price, are completely unaffected.

GLapsedgoes to a client whose policy lapsed
A policy you may have written off — let us tell you what it is worth
What they are glad to hear: a policy they wrote off may still hold value.

You held a policy with us that stopped being paid. People assume that is the end of it. Often it is not — some build a value that stays yours, some can be restarted, and a few quietly mature with nobody claiming them.

We are not asking you for anything. We would just like to tell you what you have.

HNothing heldgoes to a client with nothing in force and nothing lapsed
Is this still the right address for you?

You were a client of this branch, and our records still carry your details. We are writing to everyone we have looked after, to make sure we can still reach you, and to leave you with something worth two minutes of your time.

There is nothing to do and nothing to pay. If the address is wrong, a reply puts it right.

▶  Watch it
One thing worth knowing A life policy cannot be transferred. No agent, no broker and no other company can move it for you — if anyone suggests you move, what is being proposed is that you end this one and buy a new one, priced at your age now and underwritten on your health today. Anything a doctor has told you since this one began is priced, excluded or declined afresh; the policy you hold is already issued, and nothing about your health now can touch it. Under the Insurance Act, whoever suggests it — agent, broker or company — must discuss the advantages and the disadvantages with you first. So ask for it in writing, with both policies side by side: same sum assured, same term, same benefits, priced at the age you are now. And whatever you ever decide, keep this policy in force until any new one has actually been issued and is in your hands — a new application can take weeks, and it costs nothing to wait.
