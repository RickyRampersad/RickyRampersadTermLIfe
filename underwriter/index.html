What is already built.

The quote, the forms, the renewal history, the claim check. Every screen in it is a real branch screen carrying sample data.

Why this seat is not like the others

Most underwriting jobs are ninety per cent data entry.

This branch wrote its own software, so the work that normally eats an underwriter's day has already been taken off the desk. Here is exactly what, and it is all running today.

  • The quote fills itself

    Client, vehicle, cover history and no-claim record come out of the record. The driver's permit is photographed and read. Nobody retypes a policy number.

  • So do the forms

    The insurer's own proposal form and the customer checks are filled field by field from what we already hold, and come out as proper PDFs.

  • The renewal desk reads first

    Every term since the policy began — cover, premium, claims, what was paid and when — is on the screen before you open the file.

  • The claim arrives checked

    A claim is matched to the cover that was in force on the day of the loss, not to this year's. Gaps and lapses are flagged before anybody looks at it.

  • Duplicates are caught

    The same term entered twice, a mid-term change kept as its own record — the system finds them and says which record to correct.

  • Nothing sits quietly

    Outstanding requirements, unpaid premiums and renewals due are on a desk that chases them, with the days counted.

We are not claiming this makes underwriting easy. It means the hours you spend go on the risk, the rate and the client, which is the part you are actually good at.

Who we are looking for

Someone with a book behind them, and no room left where they are.

You have rated real risks and said no to some. You know what a surveyor's report is worth and what a broker leaves out. And you are somewhere that has stopped growing, or stopped listening.

  • Experience, genuinely

    Motor, property, liability — whichever classes you have actually underwritten. We would rather depth in two than a list of eight.

  • Judgement you can explain

    The application asks you about a risk you declined and one you wrote against the grain. That is the interview.

  • Appetite to build

    This is a new agency. How it underwrites is not settled yet, and the person in this seat gets a real say in it.

  • Service as a standard

    This branch is known for answering. An underwriter who leaves a broker waiting three days is the wrong fit, however sharp the rating.

Unparalleled service is the standard. The tools are there so you can keep it.
