The problem

The industry loses good people for avoidable reasons

Not because they weren't capable. Because nobody told them the truth up front, and nobody was accountable for their first ninety days.

If you're already licensed

You're producing, but you're doing it alone. Your manager is a name on an org chart. Nobody reviews your cases, nobody coaches your close, and the overrides go to someone you haven't spoken to this quarter.

  • You've hit a ceiling that isn't about your ability
  • Promotion looks like a favour rather than a standard
  • You're carrying admin that should never have been yours

If you're new to the industry

You've been told the income is uncapped. You haven't been told that commission takes about ninety days to become steady, that your first market is people who already know you, or that most of the job is the part before the sale.

  • Nobody explains what the work is actually like, day to day
  • "Training" turns out to mean a product manual
  • You find out it isn't for you after you've resigned from something else
Who this is for

Two roads in. Both are mapped.

We select from both, and the process branches at your first interview. Neither is the better answer.

Experienced advisors

You hold or have held a licence and you've written business. You move faster through selection because you have a record to show.

  • You want a branch that reviews your work, not one that just counts it
  • You're willing to have your production and persistency looked at honestly
  • You're interested in a path to Executive Agent and beyond — published, with figures

New to the industry

You've never held a licence. You get the full build: six modules of Discovery, real activity, and a manager working through it with you.

  • You know a lot of people and you're willing to talk to them
  • You have enough runway to get through roughly ninety days of uneven income
  • You want to be trained properly rather than thrown in
Who this isn't for

If you need a fixed salary from month one, this isn't it — advisors are paid on commission. If you can only give it evenings and weekends indefinitely, say so at the start and we'll tell you honestly what that produces. And if you'd rather not be measured, this is the wrong branch: everything here is counted, and you see the count.

What it costs you

There's no joining fee. It still costs you something.

We'd rather you see this now than discover it in week three. Nothing below is a surprise we spring on people later.

Your time, before you earn anything 10–15 hrs / week

Discovery runs about seven weeks and it is unpaid. You are not contracted yet, and you cannot sell yet.

Your licence Central Bank fee

You need a CBTT licence to sell. The application carries a fee set by the Central Bank of Trinidad and Tobago — ask us for the current figure and we'll tell you before you commit. We handle the paperwork with you.

Income runway About 90 days

Advisors are paid commission, and commission income typically takes around three months to become steady. Plan for that, not for month one. If you have under a month of savings, tell us — it doesn't disqualify you, but it changes how we'd plan your start.

The tools you already have Phone & laptop

Applications, quotes and client files are digital. A working vehicle matters too — field advisors travel to clients. If there's a gap, say so and we'll plan around it rather than discover it.

The risk that we say no Real

Not everyone who starts Discovery is offered a contract, and probation can end. That is the point of a selection process. You will be told early rather than strung along.

About the numbers you'll see in the contract

The probationary contract states targets — annualised premium, settled applications and persistency — and a twelve-month milestone. Those are the standards the contract continues against. They are not a forecast of your earnings, and we won't present them as one. What you earn depends on what you write. Ask us in the interview and we'll walk you through how commission actually works here.

Questions

The things people actually ask

If yours isn't here, ask it at the first interview. Nothing on this list is treated as a bad question.

Is this commission only?

Yes. Advisors here are paid on commission, with overrides once you move up the path. That is the trade: no ceiling on what you earn, and no floor either. It's why we ask about your runway before you start rather than after.

How long before I'm actually earning?

You cannot earn until you're licensed and contracted, which is typically two to three months from applying — Discovery is about seven weeks, then licensing.

After that, commission income typically takes around ninety days to settle into a rhythm. Anyone who tells you a precise figure for your first month is guessing.

Do I need a licence before I apply?

No. If you're new to the industry you'll apply for your CBTT licence during onboarding and we'll handle the process with you. If you already hold one, bring it — it shortens your path considerably.

I already sell insurance. Do I have to start from scratch?

No. Selection branches at the first interview. Experienced advisors are assessed on production, persistency, licence standing and why you're moving — not on the beginner's programme.

And it's confidential. We understand you may still be contracted elsewhere while you're talking to us.

Can I do this part-time?

Tell us at the start and we'll be straight with you about what part-time produces. Plenty of people transition over a few months while winding down something else, and that works. Indefinitely part-time generally doesn't, and we'd rather say so than take you through seven weeks first.

What is POP 7.0?

Guardian's assessment of potential in commission sales, administered through the company's system. It's not pass-or-fail in the way an exam is, and it isn't the whole decision. Your manager goes through the results with you afterwards — including the parts that are unflattering, because those are the useful ones.

What happens if I don't get through Discovery?

You'll be told, and you'll be told why. You'll have spent about seven weeks finding out something true about yourself and the work, without having resigned from anything. That's a considerably better outcome than discovering it in month nine.

What are market surveys, and do I have to do them?

Yes — they're part of Discovery. You have a fifteen-minute conversation with someone you already know about how they see insurance and financial planning. Nothing is sold, and they sign to confirm that.

They're how you find out whether you can hold a financial conversation with someone who knows you — which is most of the job.

Who will I be reporting to?

A named Recruiting Manager from your first interview through probation, with the Branch Manager reviewing the decisions at each gate. You'll know who both of those people are in your first week.

How do I get promoted?

Against published requirements. Executive Agent, for example, asks for four years contracted, $1.5m in annualised premium over two calendar years at 90% persistency, a client base of at least 325, an FSCP or equivalent designation, and MDRT membership within the last three years. The full document covers every position on both the sales and management tracks, and the branch tracker shows you your own production against it.

Find out in seven weeks, not in a year.
