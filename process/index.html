Part one

The sale — two client signatures, one manager

The client confirms and rates before the manager sees it, so the manager is reviewing a conversation the client has already agreed happened. Then the manager signs and adds what he can see that the advisor could not. Then the client chooses.

  1. 1
    Advisor

    Sends the prospect their link

    From the board. First name in, tap Send on WhatsApp — the link and message write themselves with the advisor’s code and a token unique to that send.

    RecordedSent · opened · watched · what they picked · booked
  2. 2
    Advisor and client

    The appointment — written live, then one tap

    Typed while they talk, figures moving as the client answers. Every recommendation carries its reason. One tap sends it — and nothing is printed or signed yet.

  3. 3
    Client — and the manager, copied

    The draft lands with both of them

    The client reads back their own facts — income, debts, dependants, what was discussed — rates the interview, and certifies it is accurate. They are not signing for the advice at this point. The email says plainly it is a draft with the direct manager for approval.

    Schedule 11 asks for exactly this: “An insurer shall endeavour to have the consumer certify the accuracy of the information it has provided.” Almost nobody does it. It also means the manager is reviewing a file the client has already agreed is correct.

    The manager receives the same draft at the same moment, marked awaiting the client’s confirmation. He can read it early; he cannot sign it yet.

    Client

    Certifies, rates, and signs the first time

    That signature says this is my situation, accurately recorded. It buys nothing and accepts nothing. It is the foundation the suitability of everything else rests on.

    CopiedThe advisor, the direct manager and the client — with the rating attached
  5. 5
    Direct manager

    Reviews, signs — and sends what he spotted to the advisor

    He does not log in. The email carries the case, the client’s certification, the star rating, and the insight panel — needs sitting in the fact find that the advisor did not raise.

    He approves the advice as it stands. Anything he spotted goes to the advisor, never to the client: it lands on the advisor’s board as work to go back for. The client’s email from him says one thing — approved, checked, signed.

    Why he does not sell to the client

    The whole proposition is that a second person reads the file and is not selling you anything. The first time a client sees that same person increase the bill, the promise dies — for the branch, not one case. Worse, advisors learn the manager tops up and start under-recommending. The needs still get raised; the advisor raises them, in a conversation, where they belong.

    Send back is not a rejection

    One line saying what is missing. The case reopens at the point it failed, the advisor fixes it, and the manager is told it has come back. The client is not told, and their confirmation stands.

  6. 6
    Client

    Gets a clean approval — and accepts or declines

    Approved, checked, signed, with the manager’s name on it. Nothing has been added and nothing is being sold. The client accepts the advice, takes part of it, or declines — and their reason is recorded as carefully as the recommendation was.

  7. 7
    Advisor

    The advisor goes back for what the manager spotted

    Those needs are on his board with the reason attached. He books the follow-up and works each one through properly — a conversation, not a bigger form. Anything he sells re-enters at step 2 and gets the same two signatures and the same check.

  8. 8
    Advisor and client

    Print, wet signature, submit

    Guardian’s six-page form plus the RR Branch addendum. The paperwork comes last, not first.

Part two

What the manager sees that the advisor did not

Every rule below reads the fact find the advisor already filled in. None of it needs new questions — it needs somebody to notice. That is the manager’s job, and this puts it in front of him in the approval email.

Ownership

A business share with nothing behind it

Business owner on the file, no buy-sell recorded. The family inherits a share they cannot sell to a partner who cannot afford to buy it.

Timing

Cover that ends with the job

Group cover recorded and no personal policy. It ends the day the employment does — usually the same day the income does.

Arithmetic

Debt costed, the income behind it not

The mortgage is covered but there is no income replacement. The house is safe and the family cannot eat in it.

Age

A child close to the education cliff

A dependant within five years of university and nothing costed against them. The cheapest year to start was last year.

Affordability

Surplus well above the premium recommended

Monthly surplus is more than three times what was recommended. Either the client was under-advised, or the advisor did not ask about the rest.

Risk

No critical illness on a sole earner

One income, dependants, and only death cover. The likelier event is not covered.

Compliance

A replacement not declared

An in-force policy on the file and new cover recommended, with no disclosure form. This one is not an opportunity — it is a stop.

Part four

The communication calendar

Nobody is ever left wondering. Each line is either a clock or a field in the branch portfolio — the ones marked in gold fire off data, not a timer.

Day 1
Welcome and what happens next The six steps, who to call, and roughly how long each one takes. Carries the complaints route — who to contact if they are ever unhappy, and how.
Every 10 days
from submission
Where your application is Starts when the application goes in, not on day one — a status email while somebody is still deciding is noise. Always names the current step. Stops the day the policy is delivered.
On issue
Your policy has been issued Fires when Issue Date appears against the policy in Branch Portfolio. There is no dispatch field in the data, and issue is the honest proxy — the policy exists and is on its way.
The 20-day clock
Delivery chasers, against a legal deadline Not an arbitrary cadence. The branch’s own document log has every agent declare delivery within 20 days under Section 155(6) of the Insurance (Amendment) Act 2020. So: day 5 a reminder, day 10 firm, day 15 copies the branch manager, day 18 flagged as heading for breach.
On delivery
Did we explain it properly? Fires when Document Delivered and Date Signed are filled in Log Book Digital. Asks whether the contract was walked through — the sections, the exclusions, how to claim, who to call. The chasers stop here.
Two weeks before their birthday, every year
Annual review Recaps every discussion and every recommendation since day one — including the ones they declined and the reason they gave. Then asks what has changed: income, dependants, debts, health, work.

Where these actually live

Branch Portfolio is the policy master — Number, Client Number, Issue Date, Status, Sum Assured, email. It has no delivery fields at all. Log Book Digital is the delivery record — that is where Document Delivered and Date Signed sit. The two join on policy number. Two things needed: the script needs access to the Log Book, and its policy number currently sits inside a free-text blob rather than its own column.

The annual review is where the addendum finally pays

Every need that never made the form is sitting in that recap with the question that opens it. A year later, with a changed circumstance beside it, it is not a cross-sell — it is a follow-up.

Part five

The safeguards

Four things that stop this failing quietly.

Silence

Nothing waits forever

Client has not certified after 3 days — reminder. After 7 the advisor rings and records a verbal certification. After 14 the case parks and shows on your dash. The manager gets the same treatment: chased at 24 hours, and at 48 it goes to the deputy.

Single point of failure

A named deputy approver

One gate and one manager means one holiday stops every case in the branch. Somebody other than you needs the authority, named now rather than on the day it matters.

Privacy

Links, not figures

A client’s income, debts, dependants and shortfall do not go in an email body copied to three people. The email carries the headline and a secure link; the figures live on the page. Schedule 11 Part C is Privacy and Data Protection, and this was the weakest point in the design.

Right of complaint

Every client email says how to complain

One line in the footer: who to contact, and that they can escalate. Market conduct expects the client to be told. Not one of our emails currently says it.

Part seven

What I need from you

Settled

1. Where the dates live — answered

There is no dispatch or acknowledgment field in Branch Portfolio. Delivery is tracked in Log Book Digital as Document Delivered and Date Signed; Issue Date in the Portfolio is the dispatch proxy. The clock is the statutory 20 days your own form already commits agents to.

Settled

2. The manager does not sell to the client — agreed

He raises the need to the advisor with the reason. The advisor books the follow-up and works the product. The independent check stays independent.

3. Does the star rating go anywhere the advisor can see?

It is on the case and on your dash. Should a low rating notify you at the time, and does the advisor see his own average?

4. Who is sales support on the thread, and the real agent list

One shared address or a named person per case. And I still need every agent’s name, code and email — nothing can go branch-wide without it.
