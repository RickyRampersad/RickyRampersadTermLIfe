Updating our records: your calls, step by step

For Sasha, Liz and Azariah. Every client on your list had an agent who is no longer with Guardian Life. You are calling to let them know, and to update what our system holds, above all their e-mail, so the branch's letter reaches them and is on record.

You are not giving advice. You tell them the one sentence, you ask, and you record. Anything about the policy itself is a call-back from a licensed agent. You never have to know the answer.

Every call, in six steps

1 · Hello
“Good morning, may I speak with {first name}? This is {your name} from the Ricky Rampersad Branch of Guardian Life, the branch that looks after your policy. This isn't a sales call.”

Someone else answers: ask when {first name} is in, and say nothing about the policy. A bad time: ask when is better, write it in Outcome.

2 · The notice, word for word
“Guardian Life of the Caribbean has accepted the resignation of your agent, {agent name}, effective 21 September 2026. {agent first name} is no longer authorised to conduct any business on behalf of Guardian Life. Your policy is not affected: it stays with Guardian Life, and our branch team looks after it.”

The same words as the letter, and nothing more: no reason, never where anyone went. If they ask why: “Guardian Life has accepted the resignation, effective 21 September, and I don't have more than that.” If they want to check it: “Please do. Guardian Life will confirm it on (868) 226-6944.”

Letter T or T1 on your list (one agent's book): read the company's notice instead, word for word, with the name and date from your list: “Guardian Life of the Caribbean terminated the contract of your agent, {agent name}, with immediate effect on {date}, as a result of an investigation. He is no longer authorised to conduct any business on behalf of Guardian Life. Your policy is not affected.” The page then asks two questions: has he been in touch since that date, and how do they pay their premium today. Tick what they say. A “yes” or “in person” is a call-back from the branch.
3 · Why you are calling
“We're updating our records so that everything from us reaches you. May I check the details we hold for you?”
4 · Their e-mail

Your list says what we hold, under “E-mail on file”:

  • None on file: “What's the best e-mail address for you?”
  • On file: “We have {e-mail}. Is that still right?” Two on file: read both and ask which one they use.
  • Bounced: “We wrote to {e-mail} on 25 September and it came back to us. What address do you use now?”
  • Sent, no answer: “We wrote to you at {e-mail} on 25 September. It comes from support@rickyrampersadbranch.com, so it may be in your junk folder. Is that still the right address?”
Spell it back letter by letter, and say the ending out loud: “gmail dot com”.

Type it on the page and tick “I read it back”. The page will not take an address that looks cut off (gmail.co) or misspelt (hotmial). No e-mail, or they would rather not: tick that, and ask whether WhatsApp suits them.

5 · Number and address
“Is this the best number to reach you on?”  “Are you still at {address on your list}?”  “And how do you like us to reach you: a call, WhatsApp or e-mail?”

Type a number or an address only if it has changed.

6 · Close
“Thank you. You'll get a letter from us by e-mail, from support@rickyrampersadbranch.com, so you have it all in writing. If you don't see it, have a look in your junk folder. And if you ever need us, the branch is on (868) 678-5921.”

Press Send on the page once. Then log the call on your tab: the try, Reached?, E-mail taken, Call-back needed, and the outcome in a few words.

You are not a licensed agent, so never
  • give advice about cover, or about keeping, changing, replacing or cancelling a policy;
  • quote a figure: a premium, a value, an amount owed, a payout;
  • promise a date or an outcome;
  • say anything about the agent beyond Guardian Life's notice, word for word (T and T1: the termination notice), or answer anything said about the branch;
  • ask for a date of birth, an ID number, a bank detail or a password;
  • read out an e-mail, a number or any detail to anyone but the client.

What happens to what you take

  • The e-mail you send from the page goes onto the client's row on the send sheet by itself, within five minutes, and waits there. Nothing goes to the client from your call.
  • When Ricky gives the go, the letter goes to that address by e-mail from support@rickyrampersadbranch.com: the record, in writing.
  • If a letter bounces, the client comes back onto your list.
  • Call-backs go to a licensed agent or the branch. You do not follow them up.
