When an agent leaves, the client stays.

How the branch looks after the clients of agents who have moved on: the film, the letters, the two doors into the review, the feedback loop, and the one number that tells us whether it worked.

The objective, in one line

Nobody leaves. Every client of a departed agent ends up with a qualified agent of their own and a filed review — and the client chooses the route.

What we assume

The agents who left will approach these clients. Some clients still like them. Nothing we send may read as hostile, defensive, or after the business.

What we do instead

Lead with something the client is glad to hear. Tell them what the law already gives them. Offer two doors and let them pick.

What counts as done

A review filed on form 2000-03-147, signed, with a named agent on the file. A conversation nobody files did not happen.

The run sheet

In the order things have to happen. Each step names who does it. Nothing in a later phase starts before the phase above it is done.

Phase 0 · Before anything — the branch manager, about thirty minutes

  1. Merge the branch to main. Then check that /vid, /templates, /your-policy, /your-policy/protected and /orphan-transition/team-review each answer. Nothing below happens on a 404.
  2. Update the Apps Script. From the Service Questionnaires sheet: Extensions → Apps Script, replace Service.gs with the repository's and add Transition.gs as a new file in the same project. Then Deploy → Manage deployments → pencil → New version. New deployment would change the address every front end holds. The pages are already wired to that deployment and ask it for its version on load; the amber “not recording yet” notice clears itself the moment the new version answers. Nothing to paste.
  3. Set up the send. Back in the sheet: Service Questionnaire → Transition: set up. That creates the Transition Send tab and installs the 8:00 digest. The hourly send is not switched on here: nothing goes to a client on its own until “go live” in step 10, however long the list sits in the tab.
  4. Clear the test rows on the questionnaire sheet, so the first real review is not beside them.

Phase 1 · The team — before any client, this afternoon

  1. Send the WhatsApp. The letters at /templates, the film at /vid, the ask: a verdict on each, and your town if you are taking assignments.
  2. Read the verdicts on the Team Feedback tab as they land. Answer every “change” and “hold” by name. A change that is right goes into build-letters.py, is regenerated, committed and merged — never typed into a letter.
  3. Build the roster from the ticks: who takes assignments, in which towns. The largest towns need more than one agent; every household goes to one.
  4. Ask Guardian Life what it records as the servicing intermediary on an in-force policy, and tell the team the answer. Until then an agent promises only “the branch does it with you”.

Phase 2 · The send — once the verdicts are answered

  1. Build the send list outside the repository with tools/letters/sendlist.py on a copy of the Branch Portfolio sheet (the departed agents' names go in a git-ignored file beside it, never in the script). One row per client: a token, the letter by the six-letter waterfall, the merge fields, and the exclusions filled in first: the departed agents' own policies (matched on full name), their households (same surname and the same address, phone or e-mail as the agent's own rows), staff e-mail addresses, and death claims. A client with the agent's surname in the agent's own book, a matured policy, or a postponed application is marked check and never sends until the check is cleared by hand. Import the CSV into the Transition Send tab.
  2. Preview, test, check, then go live. Transition: preview the letters to me sends one of each to your own inbox; it refuses, and says why, if the site is still serving the previous cut of a letter. Add the staff test rows — team members standing in as clients, marked Test = Y; never mark a client's row — and run Transition: send the Test rows now — the Test rows go whatever the hour, live or not. Read both, then read the Exclude column down once more: the departed agents' own policies and their households must each carry a reason. Only then Service Questionnaire → Transition: go live (hourly send on). From the next weekday hour, 9:00 to 17:00, the trigger sends by the Send on dates, one family a day: the notice and the action letters (T, T1, J, K, I) first, the in-force letters over the next three days by tenure, the longest first, then G and A a week on; up to sixty an hour. From day 22 the same run sends a client's own letter once more to anyone who has not answered, with a line above the greeting saying when the first went — once, never twice, and never ahead of the day's new letters. A row with anything in Exclude never sends, a row with no Send on date is held, a sent row never sends twice, and a row the sender cannot use (no e-mail, no first name, no letter for its segment) is moved to Exclude with the reason instead of being tried every hour. A mail failure is retried the next day, not the next hour. Transition: pause the hourly send stops it at any point; the Test rows still go by hand. Reply-to is a person at the branch.
  3. Call the direct-bill clients with a premium due inside thirty days this week, to tell them how to pay Guardian Life directly, with Guardian's receipt. That is the one exposure a returned receipt book creates.

Phase 3 · The clicks — daily, from the first send

  1. Every tap gets a receipt, and the branch gets a copy. The moment a client taps, they get a short thank-you e-mail naming what happens next, CC'd to the branch (TRANSITION.COPY_TO, blank = the branch manager) so a response is seen the moment it lands, not only on the page or in a digest. A tap still Open past its wait gets one internal nudge to the branch; still open at twice that, the client gets a warm “still on it” note and the branch a second, plainer one. Both stop the moment Status reads anything other than Open — that is how the branch marks a concern resolved.
  2. Watch it on one page. responses.html opens with the branch code and shows, live from the sheet: sent and waiting by letter, every tap by type, the questionnaires that came from the campaign, the team's verdicts, and first of all the taps waiting more than two working days with nobody assigned (one working day for an urgent tap). It carries a red banner while the hourly send is off, and the last runs — every batch, test, chase and stop, with the reason — at the foot. The digest e-mails the same to the branch manager, at 8:00 and at noon by default (TRANSITION.DIGEST_HOURS). On the Client Responses tab every tap except informed is a task: name the agent within two working days — town, then household, then what they hold — and fill Assigned to and Assigned on.
  3. Send the agent the brief, filled in from the sheet and the portfolio. Never to the client.
  4. The agent makes contact by the tone guide, logs approached if the client says someone has been in touch, and closes the row with a review filed on form 2000-03-147.

Phase 4 · The calls — the third of the book with no e-mail

  1. Call list order: A, E, C, F, D, then G. Not H. The call script is the shape; two lines on the sheet after every call.

Phase 5 · The scoreboard — every day

  1. The responses page, every morning: sent, taps by letter and by type, questionnaires, days from a tap to a named agent, and the one number that counts: reviews filed. The film's views and the door split, selfserve against assign, are the staffing signal.
  2. The digest at 8:00 and at noon carries the same, with what the last day's answers mean in sentences. The Monday report, by 8:00, reads the week: response by family, letter and former agent's book; every question with each answer's share; where the approaches are; when the first answer comes; follow-through and the median working days to a name; the clients to act on; the next seven days and the reminders due. Both are e-mailed to the branch manager (DIGEST_TO, WEEKLY_TO in Transition.gs) and carry client names, so they stay inside the branch.

Phase 6 · Day 8, and day 22

  1. G and A go out by their Send on dates. From day 22 the reminders go by themselves: the client's own letter once more, to anyone who has not answered, and to nobody twice. Anything the verdicts asked for is re-cut. Compliance supplies section numbers if they are ever wanted. The film's music bed is cleared for use beyond the branch site before it goes anywhere else.

The rule for the waterfall

The action letters come first: an application in progress (K), a contract not yet delivered (J), a premium due (I: a policy the sheet flags Overdue, more than sixty on its Days column). Then: Death flag → matured or maturing within six months (A) → in force with cover that ended and started again (R1, R2) → in force (F1–F5, by how long the client has held their longest policy) → lapsed (G) → nothing (no letter). A client gets one letter. Fourteen letters in four families — notice (T, T1), action (J, K, I), keep (F1–F5, R1, R2), return (G, A) — and the family is the day they go: condensed to six on 22 September so the team reads one page, then the in-force letter split into five tenure versions on 23 September, each asking for the client's view, on 24 September two check letters for clients whose cover ended and started again, and the same day letter T for every client on the book of an agent whose contract Guardian Life terminated in writing (T1 for one whose application is still in progress): it carries the company's own notice, with the name and the date read off the sheet, and goes before every other letter. On 25 September the Act's own words went onto every letter whose situation it speaks to, with the days since a contract was handed over, or an application received, worked out on the day the letter goes. The segment file is built outside the repository from the Branch Portfolio sheet (Status, Paid To Date, Days, Projected Lapse Date, Issue Date, App Received Date), the contracts-given-to-agent list and the pending list, with the maturity date from policy admin. Counts and names never come into this repository.

The taps

Every letter ends in taps, and every letter carries the door into the questionnaire: “I want an agent now. Let me tell you my concerns first”. That tap is logged and then carried straight into the review, so the client states what has gone wrong before anyone is named and the branch reads it before it assigns. Assigning is the easy part; understanding the client is the point. The other taps are chosen per situation: help me claim it, bring me my contract, finish my application, I have already paid, set me up to pay Guardian Life directly, tell me what I hold in writing, call me.

Four things no letter does

  • Names or characterises anyone who has left. “Has moved on from Guardian Life” and nothing more.
  • Tells the client they are being targeted. The Act note says what they are entitled to ask for; it does not say who might ask them.
  • Offers anything to stay. It would be matched, and it teaches the client to shop.
  • Mentions money owed. No arrears figure until cash and the deduction file are reconciled.

The agent's brief

What an agent receives with an assignment. Filled in by the branch, from the sheet and the portfolio; never sent to the client.

Open the brief template →

It must carry

The segment, so the agent opens on the right thing. What the client already said in the review, if there is one. Everyone else at the same address. The contact route that actually works. The years held.

It must not carry

Anything about the agent who left. Any arrears figure not reconciled against cash and the deduction file. Any promise the branch has not already made.

Standing rules

  • The departed agents' own policies and their households never receive a letter. The send list marks them before anything else is looked at, and a row with anything in Exclude never sends. A shared surname alone is a check, not an exclusion: Joseph and Samuel are half the parish.
  • Client data never enters the repository. The segment file, the merge file and the send list live outside it. This manual carries the method and nothing else.
  • Nothing goes out with a broken link. The film page must be on main before any letter references it. Check curl -sI first.
  • Nothing goes out before the backend is published. The pages are wired to the Service Questionnaire deployment and show an amber notice until it answers with the campaign flag. A New version must be published first, or every tap and every verdict vanishes.
  • Nothing goes out before the receivers are named. Decide who takes assignments before the send, by town.
  • Nothing goes out before the team has read it. The team review page first, the verdicts answered, then the send. A letter the agents have not seen is a letter they cannot stand behind.
  • Warm the domain. Smallest segments first, across several days.
  • Reply-to is a person. A no-reply address on a letter signed by the branch manager undoes the letter.
