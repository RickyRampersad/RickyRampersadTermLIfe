The workflow: who does what, every day

Every client of an agent who left ends up with an agent of their own, a conversation on record, and a filed review. Three roles carry it. Pick yours, or print the page for all three.

!Three rules on every call, whoever makes it

1. Nothing about the former agent beyond what the letter said: never why, never where they went, never an opinion. 2. If the client says the former agent has been in touch, or that they pay in person, thank them, say a person from the branch calls before anything else, and do not ask what was said. 3. Nothing needs to be signed or paid until they have spoken to the branch.

The notice is read word for word from the script. A client who wants to check it is given the branch's numbers and Guardian Direct's. Client names and answers never leave the board: not in WhatsApp, not in a screenshot.

Agents

the clients named to you

1Your day

MorningOpen the board (rickyrampersadbranch.com/orphanmanagement) with your agent number and password. To call is your list: every client named to you, newest first, with who named you and when. Urgent comes first: a call-back a client asked for, a pressing answer, anyone waiting too long.
Each clientRead the file, call, log it. Two working days from the naming to the first conversation: the board shows management every client with nothing logged after that.
End of dayYour work shows what you logged today and this week. Nothing should be in your head that is not on the board.

2Before you dial: read the file

Tap the name, or Details. Everything is there:

  • What they told us: their taps on the letter, their own words, the review if they filed one. Start with what they asked for.
  • Their policies: plan, in force or lapsed, paid to, cover and premium, who each policy pays, and what the records say (a premium due, cover ending, a beneficiary to confirm, a birthday).
  • The household: everyone at the address on our list, who answered, and who is still to reach. You are named on the family, not one policy.
  • History · tasks · comments: the letter, the reminder, the receipt, every call Client Support made with their notes, the open Salesforce task and its Chatter.

The brief you were e-mailed when you were named carries the same: use either. Nothing is in the old spreadsheet any more.

3The call

The call script is the letter, spoken, in the same order. Open it beside the board.

  1. Who you are: the branch that looks after their policy, and the agent now named to them.
  2. The notice, word for word from the script. Nothing more about anyone who left.
  3. What they told us: answer that first. Their question, their concern, the check they asked for.
  4. The review: the service questionnaire, form 2000-03-147, is how the file is completed. Offer to do it with them, on the call or at a visit.
  5. What happens next, in one sentence, with a date you will keep.
If they say the former agent has been in touch, or that someone suggested they change or cancel a policy: thank them, note it in their words, and tell them the branch calls before anything else. Do not advise against anyone. If they say… has the answers.

4Log it, the same day

  1. Press Log call on the row. Choose what happened: Called (you spoke), Met, No answer, Declined, Closed. Nothing saves without an outcome.
  2. Write the note: who you spoke to, what was agreed, what is next. Facts, no opinions about anyone.
  3. Save. The log goes onto the client's open Salesforce task as a Chatter post in your name. If none is open, one opens on their policy, due in two working days. The panel tells you which before you save.
What counts. A No answer is a try, not a close: three tries on three days, at different times. A task is closed only with a note of who was spoken to and what was agreed. It stays open until then, and a standard “no questions or concerns” e-mail is never the close for a client who asked for something.

A comment under History · tasks · comments also posts to the task's Chatter, so the branch and Client Support read it there too.

5The review, and the TT$200

  • A completed service questionnaire for a client named to you earns TT$200. One claim per client. Sales support sends it to Customer Service, and the claim goes to the Retention unit on the Friday transmittal after it is processed. Your Claims tab shows each one and where it stands: with Customer Service, sent, or paid.
  • A questionnaire from before the naming earns nothing. So does a staff Test row or a departed agent's own policy.
  • Make sure the questionnaire carries every policy number of the client: sales support records it on each policy in Salesforce.
  • Customer Service is followed up every 5 working days until it is processed, and the Retention unit every 3 working days until it is paid. You do not chase either. When the payment date goes in, you get one e-mail saying so, and the claim reads paid on your Claims tab.

6What the branch sees of you

  • Each named client: completed, met, spoken to, tried, or nothing logged. Nothing logged two working days after the naming is flagged red on the Agents tab.
  • Your conversations, how many clients converted (met or completed), the days from naming to first meeting, and your sign-ins. A client with nothing logged can be reassigned, and the history keeps your name.
Never
  • say anything about the former agent beyond the notice, or ask what they said;
  • promise a date for anything the branch controls (an answer from head office, a cheque);
  • leave a conversation unlogged, or log it only in your own notes;
  • share a client's name, answer or screenshot outside the board.

Client Support

the record, the calls, the naming

1Your day

MorningOpen the board with your first name and password. Your calls is your list, most urgent first: the terminated book and the action letters, then everyone the letter could not reach. Urgent holds the call-backs clients asked for and the pressing answers, across the branch.
CallsYour tab, top to bottom. Three tries on three days before a row reads not reached. Anyone who asked for a set time goes first.
After each callLog it on the board (step 4). Then, if nobody is named on them, name the household (step 5).
End of dayYour work: what you logged, the call-backs you flagged that are still open, the clients you reached with nobody named yet. Clear what you can.

The old calls sheet is not the record any more. Nothing typed there reaches the board, the reports or the chase.

2The call: six steps, no advice

The full words are in Client Support: the call. The shape:

  1. Hello: who you are, the branch that looks after their policy, not a sales call.
  2. The notice, word for word. Letter T or T1: the termination notice, and the two questions the page asks.
  3. Why you are calling: to update what we hold so that everything from us reaches them.
  4. Their e-mail, spelled back letter by letter, ticked as read back. The page refuses one that looks cut off.
  5. Number and address, only if changed; how they like to be reached.
  6. Close: the letter comes by e-mail from support@, and the branch's number.
Anything about the policy itself is a call-back from a licensed agent: “I can't look into your policy on this call. I'll have a licensed agent call you.” You never have to know the answer. Anything about the former agent, a payment handed over, or a complaint is a call-back from the branch.

3Details go through the page

  • The client's own Record it here link (the r=update page) takes the e-mail, the number, the address and how to reach them. Each lands on their record with your name, and the e-mail files itself onto their send row, held until the branch gives the go. The letter then goes to it by itself.
  • An address that bounced is never filed back. A full mailbox may be confirmed.
  • You send the client nothing yourself. Every e-mail to a client goes from support@, from the system or from Ricky.

4Log it on the board

  1. Log call on the row: Called, Met, No answer, Declined or Closed, and a note in a few words: who, what they said, what is next.
  2. Flag the call-back when there is one: licensed agent, the branch, or do not call. The note is its reason. It stays open until the agent named on the client marks the call, or the branch does.
  3. Save. The log posts to the client's open Salesforce task in your name, or opens one on their policy and contact, in progress, due in two working days. The toast says where it went.
What counts. Every step the same day on the task. A no answer is a try, not a close. A task stays open until the agent is named and the client has been spoken to. The standard “no questions or concerns” e-mail is never the close for a client who asked for something.

5Name the household

  • To assign lists everyone who answered or was reached with nobody named. Each row carries the suggested agent and why: the family's own agent first, then the 24 September plan, then the nearest agent with room, then the lightest list.
  • One press names the household, never one policy: every member on our list goes to the same agent, and the answer says how many came along. Assign the household (N) to… on a row, or tick several and Assign each as suggested.
  • The agent gets one brief by e-mail; the file says named by you; Salesforce gets the agent, the date and the campaign on each policy; and a line goes onto the client's open tasks.
  • Possible family on our books (a yellow box on the card, and the list at the top of Households): the same surname with client numbers next to each other, or the same house in the same town typed two ways. The two sit side by side: who each is, what each holds with us, our service to each, and the family's cover with what the records show is thin (someone with no life cover, a premium due, cover that ends). Compare in full opens both people's policies, service record and history. Check it, then press One household or Not family. No call is logged, and a pair marked not family never comes back. Then name the household.
  • The introduction to the client goes only to a client who answered, and only when automatic client e-mail is not on hold. Leave the box as it is.
  • A client with nothing logged by the agent after two working days: raise it with the manager, or Reassign to…; the history keeps who had them.

Never
  • give advice about cover, or about keeping, changing, replacing or cancelling a policy;
  • quote a figure, promise a date or an outcome;
  • say anything about the agent beyond the notice, or answer anything said about the branch;
  • ask for a date of birth, an ID number, a bank detail or a password;
  • read out a detail to anyone but the client, or send a client anything yourself.

Managers

the branch manager, and each unit manager over their unit

1Your day

Open onUrgent: the call-backs clients asked for (licensed agent, branch), the pressing answers, then anyone waiting past the branch's line of two working days with nobody named. Each row says what to do.
ThenDashboard: of the clients who asked for something, the share spoken to, named and completed; the late ones in red; answers and calls day by day; by book, letter, town, person; and Client Support's Salesforce tasks held, closed, closed with no note.
ThenAgents: each agent's households and clients, a progress bar, conversations and conversions, the median days to a first meeting, and nothing logged two working days after a naming, in red.
WeeklyTeam: who is signing in and how often; the points; who named whom.
ClaimsThe TT$200 claims on the board: with Customer Service, sent to Retention, or paid. Customer Service is followed up every 5 working days and Retention reminded every 3, sales support copied. A claim several reminders old is a call to Retention.

A unit manager sees the same four over their own unit: their agents, their clients, their sign-ins. Naming agents is the branch manager's and Client Support's.

2Name households in batches

  1. On Agents, the households nobody is named on come in six batches, most pressing first: urgent, finish the family, asked us, answered or reached, not answered yet, letter held.
  2. Each household carries one suggestion and the reason. One press names the household; Assign these N to <agent> names a batch; the whole batch can go as suggested.
  3. Match is the weighted view: each client's urgency, risk, value and opportunity, each agent's base on the map, the nearest agent with room. The sliders are yours; the queue re-ranks live.
  4. Every naming goes to Salesforce (agent, date, campaign) and onto the client's open tasks, and the Assignment Log keeps who named whom, when and from where.
  5. Before naming, clear Possible families to confirm at the top of Households: couples and families the address rule missed. Each pair shows the family's cover and its gaps, with the figures; Compare in full opens both people's policies, service record and history. One press joins them; then the household goes to one agent.
  6. Open a client's History for our service record: every Salesforce task done for them since the first, the days each took, how many closed by their due date, by year, by kind of work and by who served them.

The household, never a policy. A family the branch decides to split is named one member at a time with solo, and that is the exception.

3Watch the conversation, not the naming

  • A naming is the start. The measure is the first conversation inside two working days, then the meeting, then the review filed. The Agents tab shows each: days since the naming, conversations, converted, the median days to a meeting.
  • Nothing logged after two working days: ask the agent the same day. Still nothing: Reassign to… on the card, with the history kept. An agent who reports only in Salesforce Chatter reads as nothing logged until they mark the call or write the feedback field.
  • A client waiting past the line with nobody named is yours: Update on the row, and a note in your name from the four (matching you with your agent; looking into your file myself; all noted; I tried to reach you). Only you send one.

4The record and the money

  • Salesforce: every call logged on the board posts to the client's task; the Dashboard counts each person's tasks held, closed, and closed with no note. A task closed with no note of who was spoken to is a gap to raise, not a close.
  • TT$200 claims: driven by the questionnaire's dates in Salesforce. Customer Service is followed up every 5 working days until Date SQ processed by CSR is filled; the claim then goes on Friday's transmittal to the Retention unit, you and sales support copied, each agent told their own, to be paid within 3 working days, and the transmittal is reminded every 3 working days until Date SQ Comm Paid is in. A claim whose agent has left, or whose Assigned Agent and policy agent differ with no Date Assigned to Agent, is held and listed for sales support. The agent is told when it is paid. Nothing goes until you start it from the menu.
  • Points and the reward: the Team tab ranks Client Support by the period's points (reached 3, met 5, a household named 5, a conversion 10, a questionnaire after the naming 10). What a point is worth, a monthly prize or a sum per conversion, is yours to set and to announce.
  • Who is signing in: the Team tab says who opened the board, when, from what device, and who never has. A board nobody opens is the first thing to fix.

5What only you decide

  • The hold on automatic client e-mail (letters, reminders, receipts, introductions), and its release.
  • A note to a client in your name; a complaint about a notice, which the system never answers.
  • Reopening a task, changing a staff rule, the reward, the go for the TT$200 claims, the go for e-mails taken by phone.
Never
  • let a client's own words wait on a machine: a reply is a person's job, the same day;
  • characterise anyone who left, in a note, a brief or a comment that a client could one day read;
  • name a policy where the household is the unit;
  • send the branch a link to anything not yet live.
