Ricky Rampersad BranchCall script · clients with no e-mail

The call, for the third of the book we cannot e-mail

Same shape as the letter: something they are glad to hear first, the change in one neutral sentence, the two doors, and out. Under four minutes unless they want longer. Never a script read aloud — the words below are the shape, not the lines.

1 · Who is calling, and why it is not a sales call

“Good morning, is that {{first_name}}? It's {{caller}} from the Ricky Rampersad Branch of Guardian Life — the branch that looks after your policy. This isn't a sales call; I'm ringing about something on your file you should know about.” Then stop. Let them answer. If it is a bad time, take a time and end the call.

2 · The reason for the call — by segment

A · a policy has matured
“One of your policies reached the end of its term and has matured. That money is yours and it's sitting there waiting to be claimed. I'd rather you had it than not.” The only call nobody resents. Lead with this and nothing else.
E · held ten years or more
“You've held your policy with us for {{years}} years. Something people don't usually get told: your premium was fixed on the age you were when you started. You're still paying {{years}}-years-ago prices, and nobody can sell you that again — not us, not anyone.”
C · carrying waiver of premium
“Your policy carries something called a waiver of premium. In plain terms, if illness or injury stopped you working, Guardian would keep paying your premiums for you. You've been paying for that all along, and most people who have it were never told.”
F · in force
“I'm calling everyone the branch looks after to make sure we can still reach you, and to tell you what your policy is actually worth today — most people have never been shown.”
D · collected off a payroll
“Your premium comes off your salary. Those arrangements sometimes stop quietly when an employer or a payroll system changes, so we're checking yours this week. You don't need to do anything — if something's slipped, we'll find it and come back to you with the options.” The word “arrears” is not said. If they ask whether they owe money: “that's exactly what we're checking, and until we have, you don't.”
G · lapsed
“You held a policy with us that stopped being paid a while back. People assume that's the end of it — often it isn't. Some keep a value that's still yours, some can be restarted. I'd like to tell you what yours is worth.”

3 · The change, in one sentence

“You may already know your representative, {{agent_first_name}}, has moved on from Guardian Life. Your policy is completely unaffected — same cover, same premium, same beneficiaries. I wanted you to hear that from us.” No reason given. No tone. If they ask what happened: “they've moved on — I honestly don't have more than that.” And then back to their policy.

4 · The one question that matters

“Has anyone been in touch with you about moving your policy?” Not who. Just whether. Whatever they say, thank them and do not react.
If yes: “That's fine, and it's your decision. One thing worth knowing: a policy can't be transferred — what's being suggested is ending this one and buying a new one at your age now. Under the Insurance Act, whoever suggests it has to set out the advantages and the disadvantages for you first. Ask them to put it in writing. Anyone acting properly will. If it helps, I can send you a page that sets out your protections under the Act in plain words.” Then log approached on the row, and text the link: rickyrampersadbranch.com/your-policy/protected. Whether it was an agent, a broker or another company that approached them changes nothing in this answer — the same duty binds all three.
If no: move on. Do not warn them that someone might.

5 · The two doors

“There's a short review of what you hold — about fifteen minutes. You can fill it in yourself on your phone, and whoever we match you to reads it before you ever meet. Or one of our agents can go through it with you, in person or on the phone. Which would you rather?”
Themselves: read them the address slowly — “don't have an agent dot com, slash start” — or offer to text it. Log selfserve.
With an agent: “Then I'll have someone call you within two working days — a named person, not a department. Is this the best number?” Log assign.
Neither: “That's genuinely fine. Your birthday note and premium reminders carry on regardless, and we'll ask again rather than assume.” Log informed.

6 · Out

“Thank you for the time. If anything comes up, the branch answers on {{branch_phone}} — a person, not a menu.”
Never, on any call
  • Name, describe or explain the agent who left.
  • Tell a client they are in arrears. The figure is not reconciled.
  • Warn a client that someone may approach them. Ask whether someone has; nothing more.
  • Offer anything — a discount, a visit, a favour — to stay.
  • Read the segment line to a client whose row is in segment 0. Those rows are not on the call list.
After every call, on the sheet The response (selfserve / assign / informed / no answer), the best number, and whether they said anyone had approached them. Two lines. It is the only record the branch will have of this call.
