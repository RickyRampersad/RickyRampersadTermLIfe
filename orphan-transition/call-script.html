The call: hear from the client, and get their e-mail right

Client Support calls every client our letter did not reach, and every client it reached who has not answered, before any agent is named. The call is the letter, spoken: who is calling, the notice in one sentence, the reason for the call by letter, the same questions, their e-mail spelled back, what happens next, and out. The call list gives you the letter the client would have had, the facts off the sheet, the e-mail we hold and what happened to it, and the client's own answer link. Under five minutes unless they want longer. The words below are the shape, not lines to read aloud.

1 · Who is calling, and why it is not a sales call

“Good morning, is that {{first_name}}? It's {{caller}} from the Ricky Rampersad Branch of Guardian Life, the branch that looks after your policy. This isn't a sales call; I'm ringing about something on your file you should know about.” Then stop. Let them answer. If it is a bad time, take a time and end the call. If someone else answers, ask when the client is in and say nothing about the policy. If they are wary that it is really us, give them the branch number and let them ring back: a careful client is a good sign.

2 · The notice, word for word

“Guardian Life of the Caribbean has accepted the resignation of your agent, {{agent_name}}, effective 21 September 2026. {{agent_first_name}} is no longer authorised to conduct any business on behalf of Guardian Life. Your policy is not affected: same cover, same premium, same beneficiaries. I wanted you to hear that from us.” Guardian Life's notice, the same words as the letter, and nothing else: no reason, no tone, never where anyone went. If they ask what happened: “Guardian Life has accepted the resignation, effective 21 September, and I honestly don't have more than that.” If they want to check it: “Please do. The branch will confirm it on (868) 226-6461, or Guardian Direct on 226-MYGG, that is 226-6944.” Then back to their policy.
Letters T and T1 only · the book of the agent whose contract was terminated
“Your agent, {{agent_name}}, is no longer authorised to conduct any business on behalf of Guardian Life, since {{terminated_on}}. Your policy is not affected: it remains with Guardian Life, looked after by our branch team.” Since 6 October 2026 the wording of this book's notice is with the Company for review. On the phone say only that he is no longer authorised, and nothing else: not that the contract was terminated, never why, never the grounds, never where anyone went. If they ask what happened: “He is no longer authorised to act for Guardian Life, and I honestly don't have more than that.” To check it: the branch, (868) 226-6461, or Guardian Direct, 226-MYGG (226-6944). Then: “Has he been in touch with you since that date?” A yes means a person calls them back before anything else, and nothing is signed or paid until then.

4 · The questions, asked aloud

The same ones the letter asks, in the letter's order. Ask, then wait. “Not sure” is an answer, and a good one to hear.

How have we looked after you so far? very well · well · could be better. Every in-force letter opens on this.
Would you like us to confirm, in plain words, exactly what your policy pays and to whom? yes, please confirm it · I know it. A “yes” is a call-back with the answer once identity is confirmed.
If anyone ever suggests you change or replace your policy, would you like us to check it with you first, free? yes, always · someone already has, call me · I will decide myself. The Act says no agent or broker may cause you to discontinue or replace a policy without first discussing the advantages and the disadvantages with you. Whatever they answer, thank them and do not react; never warn that someone might.
Would you like our branch team to keep looking after your policy? yes, keep looking after it · let's talk it through first. Always last.
By letter: F2 and F4, has life moved on? · F1, a plain-words walk-through? · F3, what it has built? · F5, anything more the cover should do? · G, find out, free, what it still holds? · J and T, has your contract reached you? · K and T1, is anything still outstanding? · T, has he been in touch since that date, and how do you pay your premium today?
Then: what is the best way to reach you, and, if a call is coming, morning, afternoon or evening?

5 · Their e-mail, spelled back

The list says what we hold for them, in the column E-mail on file, and what happened to it. Only ever to the client themselves.

None on file
“So that our letters reach you and you have everything in writing, what is the best e-mail address for you?”
On file, never used · or two on file
“We have an e-mail for you, {{email}}. Is that still the right one?” Two on file: read both and ask which one they use. Cut off on file (it ends “gmail.co” or stops short): ask for it in full.
Bounced
“We wrote to you on 25 September at {{email}}, and it came back to us. What address do you use now?” “Mailbox full” on the list: it came back because the mailbox was full; ask whether that is still the one to use, or another.
Sent 25 September, no answer yet
“We wrote to you on 25 September at {{email}}. Did it reach you? It comes from support@rickyrampersadbranch.com, so it may be sitting in your junk folder.” Then the questions, if they have not answered them, and confirm the address.
Every time: spell it back letter by letter, and say the ending out loud: “gmail dot com”. The slips to listen for are an ending cut short (gmail.co) and a letter swapped (hotmial). One address only, and theirs: a family address is fine if that is the one they read; never an agent's or an office one. Then: “We use it only for your policy: our letter, your receipts and your reviews, never for anything else, and it is never shared. Our letter comes from support@rickyrampersadbranch.com; if you do not see it, look in junk and add that address to your contacts.” No date for the letter: it goes when the branch releases it. No e-mail, or would rather not give one: that is fine; tick it, and ask whether WhatsApp suits them.

6 · Record it while they are on the line

Open the client's own link from the list (Record the answers here) on your phone or screen. It ends in r=phone: that is the caller's copy of the page, and nothing is recorded until you press Send. Every question the letter would have asked is on that page. Tick what they told you, exactly as they said it, add how to reach them and when, type the e-mail into the box at the foot and tick that you read it back (or tick that they have none), put your name in the caller box once (your phone keeps it), and press Send my answers once. The page will not send an address that looks cut off or misspelt, or one you have not read back. It all lands on the Client Responses tab under that client, the e-mail with your name beside it, and in the reports, the same as a tap on a letter. Nothing goes to the client from this page, not a receipt and not a letter, so repeat back what you ticked: on this call you are the receipt.
If they would rather have an agent now: tick nothing for that; say the branch reads their concerns first and then matches them to the agent who fits, with no date promised for the match, and note it in Outcome on the list. If they say someone has already approached them: tick “Someone already has. Call me”, say a person calls them back before they decide anything, and offer the page that sets out their protections in plain words: ricky rampersad branch dot com, slash your policy, slash protected. If they say the former agent told them something else (that they are still with Guardian Life, or anything about the branch or the people in it): do not argue and do not answer it. “I can only tell you what Guardian Life's notice says, and you are welcome to check it with the branch on (868) 226-6461, or with Guardian Direct on 226-MYGG, that is 226-6944.” Tick “yes” on whether the former agent has been in touch, and write in Outcome what they were told, when and how (a call, a visit, Facebook, WhatsApp), in their words, never yours.

7 · What happens next, in their words

“Here's what happens now. A person at the branch reads what you've told me today. If you asked for a call, it comes within two working days, at the time you chose. Once your file has been read, we introduce the agent who fits it, in writing, with a name and a number.” The same four steps every letter carries. Nothing else is promised, and no date for the agent.

8 · Out

“Thank you for the time. If anything comes up, the branch answers on {{branch_phone}}: a person, not a menu.”
Never, on any call
  • Describe or explain anyone who left, or answer anything said about the branch. Guardian Life's notice (on T and T1, the termination notice), word for word, and nothing beyond it.
  • Tell a client they are in arrears, or quote a money figure. Days and dates only.
  • Warn a client that someone may approach them. Ask whether someone has; nothing more.
  • Offer anything, a discount, a visit, a favour, to stay.
  • Read a T line to a client who is not on that book, or call a row the list holds for a person to check first.
  • Read out an e-mail, a number or any detail of the policy to anyone but the client.
  • Ask for a date of birth, an ID number, a bank detail or a password. This call never needs them.
After every call, on the list The try (first, second, third, with the date), reached (yes, no answer, wrong number), and the outcome in a few words. The answers and the e-mail live on the client's link, sent while they were on the line; the list is the record that the call was made. Three tries on three different days, at different times of day, before a row is marked not reached.
