Ricky Rampersad BranchCall script · clients with no e-mail on file

The call, for the clients we cannot e-mail

The call is the letter, spoken: who is calling, the notice in one sentence, the reason for the call by letter, the same questions, what happens next, and out. The call list gives you the letter the client would have had, the facts off the sheet, one line on why we are calling, and the client's own answer link. Under five minutes unless they want longer. The words below are the shape, not lines to read aloud.

1 · Who is calling, and why it is not a sales call

“Good morning, is that {{first_name}}? It's {{caller}} from the Ricky Rampersad Branch of Guardian Life, the branch that looks after your policy. This isn't a sales call; I'm ringing about something on your file you should know about.” Then stop. Let them answer. If it is a bad time, take a time and end the call.

2 · The notice, in one sentence

“Your representative, {{agent_first_name}}, has moved on from Guardian Life. Your policy has not: same cover, same premium, same beneficiaries. I wanted you to hear that from us.” No reason, no tone. If they ask what happened: “they've moved on, and I honestly don't have more than that.” Then back to their policy.
Letters T and T1 only · the book of the agent whose contract was terminated
“Guardian Life of the Caribbean terminated the contract of your agent, {{agent_name}}, with immediate effect on {{terminated_on}}, as a result of an investigation. He is no longer authorised to conduct any business on behalf of Guardian Life. Your policy is not affected: it remains with Guardian Life, looked after by our branch team.” The company's own notice, and nothing else. Never why, never the grounds. Then: “Has he been in touch with you since that date?” A yes means a person calls them back before anything else, and nothing is signed or paid until then.

3 · The reason for the call — by letter

The list says which letter. The facts beside it (with us since, paid to, days, the dates) are the ones the letter would have printed: use them, and never a money figure.

Notice · T, T1 · the notice above is the reason; then the questions
Action · something to finish
J · contract handed over for delivery, never acknowledged
“Your policy is in force. Our record shows your contract was handed over for delivery on {{collected_on}} and was never acknowledged as received. Our own standard is delivery within 28 days. Did it reach you?” If not: that is ours to put right; we bring it by hand and go through every page. If they paid anything when it was brought: it counts as paid to Guardian Life, the receipt is Guardian Life's, keep it.
K · application still in progress
“Your application is still in progress, and until the policy is issued the cover you applied for isn't in place yet. Once the last requirement is in, the Act gives Guardian Life twenty business days to issue it. Whatever is still needed, a medical, a document, a signature, we bring to you. Is anything you were asked for still outstanding?”
I · a premium showing as due
“A premium is showing as due on your policy, paid to {{paid_to}}. It's usually one of three simple things: paid and not yet recorded, a change in how you pay, or it slipped past. Which is it for you?” “Arrears” is never said. A premium handed to a representative counts as paid: ask for the receipt, and nothing can be forfeited without a written notice and twenty business days.
Keep · in force · the feedback letter, by how long they have been with us
F1 · under two years
“Your policy is still new, and nothing about it has changed. How has the start been for you? And would a plain-words walk-through of what it does help?”
F2 · two to three years
“A couple of years in, nothing has changed. Has life moved on since you took it out: a new home, a new job, someone new in the family?”
F3 · three to five years
“Everything your policy has built over these years is still yours. Would you like to know what it has built so far?”
F4 · five to ten years
“You've trusted us with your cover for years, and every one of them still counts. How have we done over that time?”
F5 · ten years or more
“More than ten years with us, and every year of it is still yours. Thank you. Your view matters most: how have we looked after you? And is there anything more you'd want your cover to do now: family, retirement, health?”
R1, R2 · cover that ended and started again
“Our records show cover of yours that ended and started again along the way. We'd like to go through your file with you, free: what carried over, what started again, and who your policy pays.” Never how it happened, never by whom.
Return · lapsed or matured
G · lapsed
“You held a policy with us that stopped being paid a while back. People assume that's the end of it, and often it isn't: some keep a value that's still yours, some can simply start again. May we find out which is true of yours, free?”
A · matured
“One of your policies has reached the end of its term. That money is yours, and we bring the form and walk it through with you.” The only call nobody resents. A cheque in their name must reach them within five business days of anyone receiving it for them.

4 · The questions, asked aloud

The same ones the letter asks, in the letter's order. Ask, then wait. “Not sure” is an answer, and a good one to hear.

How have we looked after you so far? very well · well · could be better. Every in-force letter opens on this.
Would you like us to confirm, in plain words, exactly what your policy pays and to whom? yes, please confirm it · I know it. A “yes” is a call-back with the answer once identity is confirmed.
If anyone ever suggests you change or replace your policy, would you like us to check it with you first, free? yes, always · someone already has, call me · I will decide myself. The Act says no agent or broker may cause you to discontinue or replace a policy without first discussing the advantages and the disadvantages with you. Whatever they answer, thank them and do not react; never warn that someone might.
Would you like our branch team to keep looking after your policy? yes, keep looking after it · let's talk it through first. Always last.
By letter: F2 and F4, has life moved on? · F1, a plain-words walk-through? · F3, what it has built? · F5, anything more the cover should do? · G, find out, free, what it still holds? · J and T, has your contract reached you? · K and T1, is anything still outstanding? · T, has he been in touch since that date, and how do you pay your premium today?
Then: what is the best way to reach you, and, if a call is coming, morning, afternoon or evening?

5 · Record it while they are on the line

Open the client's own link from the list (Record the answers here) on your phone or screen. Every question the letter would have asked is on that page. Tick what they told you, exactly as they said it, add how to reach them and when, and press Send my answers once. It lands on the Client Responses tab under that client, and in the reports, the same as a tap on a letter. Then repeat back what you ticked: no receipt can go by e-mail to a client who has none, so on this call you are the receipt.
If they would rather have an agent now: tick nothing for that; say the branch reads their concerns first and then matches them to the agent who fits, with no date promised for the match, and note it in Outcome on the list. If they say someone has already approached them: tick “Someone already has. Call me”, say a person calls them back before they decide anything, and offer the page that sets out their protections in plain words: ricky rampersad branch dot com, slash your policy, slash protected.

6 · What happens next, in their words

“Here's what happens now. A person at the branch reads what you've told me today. If you asked for a call, it comes today or tomorrow, at the time you chose. Once your file has been read, we introduce the agent who fits it, in writing, with a name and a number.” The same four steps every letter carries. Nothing else is promised, and no date for the agent.

7 · Out

“Thank you for the time. If anything comes up, the branch answers on {{branch_phone}}: a person, not a menu.”
Never, on any call
  • Name, describe or explain anyone who left. Letters T and T1 carry the company's notice, and nothing beyond it.
  • Tell a client they are in arrears, or quote a money figure. Days and dates only.
  • Warn a client that someone may approach them. Ask whether someone has; nothing more.
  • Offer anything, a discount, a visit, a favour, to stay.
  • Read a T line to a client who is not on that book, or call a row the list holds for a person to check first.
After every call, on the list Caller, called on, reached (yes, no answer, wrong number), and the outcome in a few words. The answers themselves live on the client's link, ticked while they were on the line; the list is the record that the call was made. Three tries on three different days before a row is marked not reached.
