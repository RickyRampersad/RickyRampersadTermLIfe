Part one

For advisors

Nine steps from a name in your phone to a policy in their hands. Steps one to three are live today. Four onward run on the existing system while the new flow is finished — where that matters, it says so.

  1. 1

    Send the link

    Open your board, type their first name and WhatsApp number, tap Send on WhatsApp. The link and the message write themselves with your code on them.

    The page opens in your name, with your registration on it. Your QR code is on the same card — put it on your business card and anyone who scans it lands on your page and counts as yours.

    Send it the night before, not the morning of. People watch a film in the evening. Below half your links being opened is almost always a timing problem, not a content one.

  2. 2

    Watch what they do with it

    Your board fills in by itself: sent · opened · watched · picked · booked. You are emailed the first time they open it, and again when they say what they want. Your unit manager is copied on both.

    Under each name is a line telling you what to do — ring them, send one line, try a different hour. Work from that line.

    The best call you will make all week is to someone who picked two or three needs and did not book. They have told you what they are worried about and stopped. Ring them the same day.

  3. 3

    Go to the appointment knowing what they want

    Before you sit down you already know what they tapped and, if they used it, the figure they saw. Open on that: “You said the mortgage and the children. Let us start there.”

    Bring nothing to sell. The numbers come first.

  4. 4

    Write the fact find while you talk

    Type it live — income, expenses, debts, dependants. The client watches their own arithmetic happen, which is the whole difference between this and a form filled in afterwards from memory.

    Every recommendation carries its reason, in plain words. If you cannot write the reason, do not make the recommendation. The reason is the file.

    Replacing an existing Guardian policy? The form will say so. Talk the client through what they gain and what they give up, then complete and sign the disclosure form at the back of the pack, with them, before the application is filled in.

  5. 5

    One tap sends it

    Nothing prints, nothing is signed, nothing reaches the client yet. It goes to your direct manager, and you get your own copy.

  6. 6

    Your manager reads it and signs — or sends it back

    He works from his phone. If he sends it back, you get one line saying what is missing. That is not a rejection. Reopen the case, fix that one thing, resubmit — he is told it has come back and reads the difference rather than the whole case again. The client is not told any of this happened.

  7. 7

    The client gets their own copy

    Plain language, to their own inbox: their needs, what was recommended, and why. They can agree, ask to discuss, or decline — and their reason is recorded as carefully as the recommendation was.

    Saying no is a legitimate answer here, and it is written down as one.

  8. 8

    Print, sign, submit

    Guardian’s six pages plus the RR Branch addendum. Wet signature on the day. The paperwork comes last, not first.

  9. 9

    The addendum is your next appointment

    Four needs go on Guardian’s form. Everything else the fact find found is set out on the addendum with the question that opens it — the business with no buy-sell, the mortgaged rental, the card nobody insured, the child with nothing costed.

    Put it in your bag. Book the follow-up before you leave.

    You must deliver the contract within twenty days. That is the declaration you sign in the document log, under section 155(6). Diarise it the day the policy is issued.

Part one and a half

The four-point check

Read this once and then do it before every submit, until the new build is merged. It takes about a minute.

The form you are using works. Nothing is broken and nothing is being lost. What it does not yet have is the checker — the panel in the new build that reads your own case back and tells you what is missing before it goes anywhere. Until that is merged, you are the checker. Four things, in this order.

  1. 1

    Every recommendation has a reason written against it

    Not a product name. The line that answers “why did you sell them that, at that amount?” Read each row back and check there is one.

    This is the single most important item on the whole file. A recommendation with no reason is not advice, it is a sale.

  2. 2

    Everything they turned down has their reason recorded

    In their words, not yours. “Cannot afford it this year”, “wants to wait until the mortgage is done”.

    This protects the client, and it protects you if the risk they declined actually happens.

  3. 3

    If cover is being replaced, it is declared

    Any existing Guardian policy going away or being reduced. Declare it and sign the disclosure with the client.

    This is the one with real consequences. An undeclared replacement comes back as Not Proceeded With, and it is a conduct matter rather than a paperwork slip. If you are not sure whether it counts, declare it.

  4. 4

    The plan can actually be issued as written

    Age inside the product’s band, occupational class accepted, and the DI rider within its own age range. The new build checks this for you; today it is your eye.

    Getting this wrong does not fail quietly — the case comes back from head office and the client is told there is a delay.

Your manager is the safety net, and this is exactly why step six exists. He checks the same four things before he signs. Two people checking is why we can tell a client somebody who is not selling them anything read the file — and it is the reason a missing reason gets caught today rather than in six months.

Part two

For managers

You are the reason the branch can say a second person checks the work. That only holds if you do it quickly and you do not sell.

  1. 1

    Decide from the email

    The whole case is in the message — the client’s numbers, every recommendation, and the reason written against each one. Three buttons: Approve & sign, Send back, Decline. You do not log in anywhere.

    Approving signs it in your name with the date and time, and writes that signature onto the fact find itself.

  2. 2

    Check four things, and only four

    • Does the cover actually match the need?
    • Can this person afford the premium?
    • Is the reason for it written down?
    • Is anything being replaced — and was that explained?

    If all four hold, approve it. You are not re-doing the fact find.

  3. 3

    Send back rather than decline, nearly always

    Send back is coaching: one line saying what is missing, and the case reopens where it failed. Decline closes the case and nobody is being asked to fix anything — keep it for cases that should not have been written at all.

  4. 4

    Do not add to the sale

    If you spot a need the advisor missed, it goes to the advisor, never to the client. He books the follow-up and works it properly.

    This is the one rule that protects everything else. The branch tells every client that a second person reads the file and is not selling them anything. The first time a client sees that same person increase their bill, the promise is gone — for the branch, not for one case.

  5. 5

    Read the dash at five

    The daily dash opens on the four numbers that decide your evening, then the signing queue oldest first, every advisor ranked by needs found, and coaching lines only where a number says something.

    The 5pm message writes itself from the same figures. Pick what goes in, tap Send on WhatsApp.

    Needs found per fact find is the number that matters. Not volume. It is the difference between an advisor making a sale and an advisor building a relationship, and it is the only figure on that page worth coaching against.

Part five

When it goes wrong

The client says they never got the link

Check the number on the board. Trinidad numbers work as seven digits, 868 plus seven, or 1868 plus seven — all three are handled. If it still shows nothing after an hour, send it again from the board rather than forwarding the old message; a forwarded link still records, but it will not carry their name.

The board shows somebody twice

Two separate sends. Each send makes its own row so you can tell which message they actually opened. Work the newest one.

An advisor gets no emails

Access tab: is the row Active, and is the email right? Nothing is sent to anybody who is not active there — but everything still records on the sheet, so nothing is lost while it is fixed.

A manager is away

Cases go to the named deputy after 48 hours. Until the new flow is built, the branch manager approves. Tell the desk when you are out.

“Is the form we are using safe to keep using?”

Yes. It captures everything and nothing is lost. What it does not have yet is the panel that reads your case back and flags what is missing — so until the new build is merged, do the four-point check above before you submit, and your manager does the same before he signs.

Something looks wrong on a page

Say so the same day, with a screenshot and what you were doing. Everything here was built in a week and the things that get fixed are the things that get reported.

Part six

The rules we set, and why

These were open questions. They are answered now, so nobody has to guess. Each one can be changed — but change it deliberately.

Settled

A manager may decline outright

Three outcomes, not two: approve, send back, decline. Send back is coaching and is what you should use nearly always. Decline is for a case that should not have been written, and it closes the case rather than asking anybody to fix it.

Settled

The deputy is the Assistant Branch Manager

Kerwyn Ramroach. A case unread after 48 hours goes to him. One manager and one gate means the branch stops when that manager is away, and that is not an acceptable way to run a queue.

Settled

Chasing runs 24 then 48 hours

A reminder at 24 hours, the deputy at 48. The dash shows anything over a day in red so it is visible before it is chased.

Settled

The client’s in-email signature confirms the advice, not the contract

It records that they read the recommendation and what they decided. The contract is the wet signature on Guardian’s form. The two are not interchangeable and the emails say which is which.

Settled

Sales support is one shared address

rickyrampersadsalessupport@myguardiangroup.com — already the address the system uses. A named person per case creates a gap the day that person is on leave.

Settled

The star rating is a coaching signal, never a score

It sits on the case and on the dash. It is not published, not ranked and not read out. An advisor whose rating is public will coach the client before the email arrives, and then it measures nothing.

Settled

Replacement covers Guardian policies

One line, no citations, and it does not block submission: the policy is named and the disclosure form must be signed. Other insurers are out of scope by decision.
