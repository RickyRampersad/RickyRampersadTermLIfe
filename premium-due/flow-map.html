How a premium-due case moves

One page, drawn out end to end. The client is written to until day 60; then the letters stop and a manager is put on the case in two steps — first a commitment, then the feedback. Every repeat has one job and one way to end it, so nothing is ever asked twice and nobody is chased for something they have already done.

Every loop has exactly one way to end

This is what keeps it from ever becoming three emails saying the same thing.

The client One answer stops everything Any reply — or a payment — ends the reminders on the spot. Nobody who has acted is chased again.
Phase 1 · commitment The four commitments close it The 3-day chase asks only what’s still blank, and stops the moment the last one is in. Then the thank-you, once.
Phase 2 · feedback The report back closes it A different clock, 7 days, begun only after he commits — so the two phases never overlap and never double up.
Why the client is copied on all of it

From day 60 the client is on every email to the manager and on every reminder that follows. There is no version of their case they cannot see. They stop receiving “please pay” letters and start watching their policy being worked — which is the difference between a client who feels chased and a client who feels held.

The manager’s private, commercial calls — the decision to allow a lapse, a reassignment, how likely the policy is to be saved — are asked separately and never appear on anything the client is copied on.
