Why cancel is on the list

Because a client who tells us beats a client who goes quiet. A stated intention to cancel is a conversation we can still win, and if we cannot, we close it properly instead of watching it lapse. Silence is the outcome we have no answer to.

Accountability

Both sides are on a clock now

When a policy lapses, two explanations usually arrive at once: the agent let it go, and the manager never came back to me. Only one of them is ever true.

ClockDeadlineIf it is missed
Client respondsAny time after day 60Chased every 5 days, manager asked to reach out. Stops on any answer.
Agent files the caseDay 65Emailed with the manager copied. Case reads Waiting on [agent]. Appears on their sign-in gate.
Manager responds5 days from filingEmailed every 3 days, branch manager copied from the second. Appears on their sign-in gate.
Managers meet a gate too

An agent who files on time and then waits three weeks for an answer has been let down, not the other way round. So a manager with overdue responses does not reach their dashboard either until they commit to clearing them — and that commitment is written to the case under their name, exactly as it is for agents.

Before you attribute a lapse to anyone, look at the Late — either side filter. It splits the count into manager-side and agent-side. A unit with six late cases where five are sitting on a manager's desk is not an agent performance problem.

By role

What changes for you

Agent

You own the contact

  • Survey out inside the 45-day window — don't wait until 59
  • File the retention case by day 65, fact find attached. That date is now measured
  • Log every contact, including the ones that went nowhere
  • Check the thread before you reach out — if the client answered two days ago, a chase reads as though nobody is listening
Unit Manager · ABM

You own the decision

  • Answer within 5 days of filing. Past that you are chased every 3 days and the BM is copied
  • You are copied on every 60-day email for a reason — reach out to those clients yourself
  • Give an instruction and a deadline, not just approval
  • Watch for cases that are always filed at day 88
Sales Support

You own the flow

  • Route incoming surveys and client replies to the right agent the same day
  • Chase pending underwriting — that is written business sitting in suspense
  • Flag recent lapses for win-back while the window is open
  • Spot the multi-policy clusters: one failed mandate, not four separate problems
Branch Manager

You own the pattern

  • The scorecard shows who works the funnel and who lets it age
  • Premium in arrears is renewable income walking out
  • Repeated Allow lapse — documented from one unit is a conversation
  • The late split is the fastest read on whether the branch is responsive

The point

Ninety days is not very long

A policy that lapses is not a number coming off a report. It is a family that thought they were covered and finds out they are not, usually at the worst possible moment. Everything they paid in stops working for them, and where health has changed since they took it out, the cover they had may not be available again at any price.

Almost none of these are lost because the client wanted to stop. They are lost to a bank mandate that failed, a card that expired, a bad month, a message that went to an inbox nobody reads.

All of those are fixable, and only inside the window. That is the whole job, and thirty days is a great deal more room to do it in than fifteen.
