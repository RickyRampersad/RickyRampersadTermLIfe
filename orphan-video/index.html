Things people find out too late

Every one of these is sitting in somebody's policy on this book right now, unmentioned.

⚑

A waiver of premium

A benefit that pays your premiums for you if illness or injury stops you working, so the cover stays in force while you recover. It is one of the most commonly held riders on this book and one of the least known.

⚑

A policy that has matured

It reached the end of its term and the money became payable. If nobody claimed it, it is still sitting there. It is yours.

⚑

Value in one that lapsed

A policy that stopped being paid does not always stop being worth something. Some build a value that stays yours, and some can be restarted.

One thing worth protecting

Your premium is priced on the age you were when you started — not your age now. A policy taken out years ago is still charging years-ago prices, and no company can sell that price back to you. It is the part of a life policy people give away without realising they owned it.

Which is why we would rather you had an agent of your own

If anyone asks you to move this policy

You do not have to argue with them. You only have to ask for one thing.

The Insurance Act · Trinidad and Tobago
An agent, agency, broker, brokerage or a sales representative shall not … cause a policyholder to replace a long-term insurance policy without first discussing the advantages and disadvantages of the discontinuance of the policy.

So ask for it in writing. “Set out for me what I gain and what I give up.” That discussion is your right, not a favour — and anyone acting properly will put it in front of you without hesitation.

A policy cannot be transferred

No agent can take your Guardian Life policy anywhere. If you were to follow one, what is actually being proposed is that you end this policy and buy a different one — priced at your age now, underwritten on your health now, with a fresh period during which a claim can be contested, and giving up whatever this one has built.

And only a registered person may advise you

No individual may carry on business as an agent or sales representative in Trinidad and Tobago unless they are registered under the Act. You are entitled to ask anyone who advises you which company their certificate of registration names today — and to ask us the same.
