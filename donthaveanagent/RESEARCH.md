# The orphan policy problem — research brief

Everything on donthaveanagent.com is built on these findings. Every statistic quoted on
the site is listed here with its source, so nothing on the page is a number somebody made
up in a marketing meeting.

Compiled August 2026.

---

## 1. The scale — this is not a niche

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| **More than 40%** of all life insurance policies are orphans, with no active agent servicing the policyholder | ThinkAdvisor, cited in [Sureify](https://www.sureify.com/press/a-golden-opportunity-for-life-insurers-the-orphaned-policyholder/) / [Coverager](https://coverager.com/a-golden-opportunity-for-life-insurers-the-orphaned-policyholder/) |
| **1 in 4** life insurance agents plan to exit the business within five years | ThinkAdvisor, same |
| Only **4%** of millennials are interested in or pursuing an insurance career | The Hartford, *A Generation of Leaders*, cited same |

The third number is why this gets worse, not better. Agents are leaving faster than they
are being replaced, and every departure creates a new cohort of policyholders nobody is
calling.

**How this shapes the product:** the landing page opens by telling the visitor they are in
the majority, not the exception. Shame is the single biggest reason an orphaned client
does nothing — they assume they were dropped because they weren't worth keeping. They
weren't dropped. Their agent left, and 40% of policyholders are in the same position.

## 2. What "no agent" actually looks like

The J.D. Power **2025 U.S. Individual Life Insurance Study** is the strongest evidence
available, because it measures the relationship rather than the policy.

| Finding | Number |
|---|---|
| Relationships classified as **disengaged or transactional** | **58%** |
| **Disengaged** — no agent or advisor interaction in **more than 3 years** | **43%** |
| Transactional | 15% |
| Describe the relationship as **trusted** — consistent communication, best practices followed | **only 19%** |
| Satisfaction when best practices *are* met | **795** — a **253-point** gap |
| Satisfaction lift when the customer recalls any communication in the past 12 months | **+50 points** |
| Direct-from-carrier satisfaction (696) versus buying through an agent or broker | **57 points higher** for direct |

Sources: [J.D. Power](https://www.jdpower.com/business/press-releases/2025-us-individual-life-insurance-study/),
[Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/life-insurance/life-insurance-satisfaction-steady-but-brand-gaps-widen--j-d--power-552655.aspx),
[InsuranceNewsNet](https://insurancenewsnet.com/innarticle/agents-risk-losing-clients-as-direct-life-channels-score-higher-in-satisfaction).

That last row is the uncomfortable one for our industry: buying from a website now beats
buying from a human being on satisfaction. The only thing an agent has that a website
doesn't is the follow-up — and 43% of us aren't doing it.

**How this shapes the product:** the promise on the site is a specific, checkable one — a
named human, a real review, and a date for the next one. Not "excellent service".

## 3. What it costs the policyholder

An unwatched policy doesn't announce itself. It degrades quietly.

| Finding | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Policyholders who say they **completely understand** their policy | **15%** | [Western & Southern](https://www.westernsouthern.com/life-insurance/life-insurance-literacy-in-america) |
| Don't know the difference between term and permanent | 46% | same |
| Say they know how their policy works | 57% | same |
| **Never updated** their life insurance after a major life change | **more than 90%** | Western & Southern survey of 1,009 Americans, via [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/more-90-americans-don-t-220207253.html) |
| Regularly consult their insurer or advisor about policy details | **26%** | same |
| **Rarely or never** review their policies | **40%** | same |
| Life insurance policies whose death benefit is **never claimed** | **1 in 600** | Consumer Reports, via [Triple-I](https://www.iii.org/article/unclaimed-life-insurance-benefits) |

The eight life events the survey tested — marriage, buying a home, having children, paying
for college, starting a business, caring for aging parents, grandchildren, retirement —
are exactly the events that change how much cover somebody needs. Ninety per cent of
people went through one and told nobody.

**How this shapes the product:** the life-events question is the single highest-yield
question on the form. It is asked early, as a simple set of taps, and it is the one most
likely to surface real unmet need.

## 4. The beneficiaries are not ready

NAIC consumer survey, 1,000+ respondents across Gen Z, Millennials and Baby Boomers.

| Finding | Number |
|---|---|
| Know they are named as a beneficiary | **50%** |
| Know the **name of the insurance company** | **29%** |
| Know the **benefit amount** | **25%** |
| Know **where the policy is kept** | 23% Gen Z · 29% Millennials · 41% Boomers |
| Feel prepared for the beneficiary role | 22% Gen Z · 30% Millennials · 39% Boomers |
| Recovered through the NAIC Policy Locator since 2016 | **$765 million+** |

Source: [NAIC via PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-naic-survey-shows-most-life-insurance-beneficiaries-arent-ready-for-their-roles-300994471.html).

Wider recovery figures: regulator settlements have driven **$9.7 billion** in payments to
beneficiaries, and NAIC processes helped consumers collect **over $6 billion** between 2016
and 2023 ([Triple-I](https://www.iii.org/article/unclaimed-life-insurance-benefits),
[Policygenius](https://www.policygenius.com/life-insurance/unclaimed-life-insurance-act/)).

**How this shapes the product:** "Does the person you named know where to find this?" is
asked plainly, and a *Policy Location Record* — a one-page sheet the client gives their
family — is offered free at the end. It costs us nothing and it is the single most useful
thing on the site.

## 5. Trinidad & Tobago — the local frame

- The **Insurance Act, 2018** governs the market and was written to strengthen policyholder
  protection ([Central Bank of T&T](https://www.central-bank.org.tt/resources/insurance-act-2018/),
  [full text](https://www.ttparliament.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/a2018-04g.pdf)).
- **Section 197(5)** deals with unclaimed monies — sums legally payable by an insurer that
  go **unclaimed for seven years** after the policy's maturity date. Insurers must report
  these to the Central Bank on a standardised return
  ([circular](https://www.central-bank.org.tt/resources/circular-letter-standardized-reporting-of-unclaimed-monies-under-section-1975-of-the-insurance-act-2018/)).

Seven years is the number worth saying out loud. A matured endowment nobody told you about
sits there, and the clock runs.

**How this shapes the product:** the site states plainly that money can go unclaimed under
T&T law and that a review checks for it. It also grounds the service in local regulation
rather than borrowed American statistics.

## 6. What actually happens when an agent leaves

Consistent across every source:

1. **The policy stays completely valid.** The insurer's obligations — premium collection,
   servicing, paying claims — are unaffected. The contract is with the company, never with
   the individual agent.
2. The insurer is expected to reassign the policy to another agent (in some markets called
   an *allottee agent*) and to tell the policyholder who that is.
3. In practice, the reassignment is often administrative. A name changes in a system.
   Nobody calls.

Sources: [Alea](https://alea.care/insurance-explained/orphan-policy),
[Tata AIA](https://www.tataaia.com/blogs/life-insurance/everything-you-need-to-know-about-orphan-life-insurance-policy.html),
[IRDAI](https://intranet.irdai.gov.in/web/policy-holder/servicing-of-life-insurance-orphan-policies),
[Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/insight/letters-to-the-editor/opinion-life-insurance-industrys-neglect-of-orphan-policyholders-poses-long-term-risks/).

**How this shapes the product:** point 1 is the first thing the visitor reads, in the
largest type on the page. The most common fear an orphaned client carries is that their
policy died with the relationship. It didn't. Leading with reassurance rather than alarm
is both true and disarming — and it is why this page can afford to be calm where most
insurance marketing shouts.

## 7. The emotional map

Drawn from the consumer research above and from how orphaned policyholders describe the
experience. This is what the copy has to answer.

| What they feel | What they don't say out loud | What the page must do |
|---|---|---|
| **Forgotten** | "I suppose I wasn't a big enough client." | Name the real reason immediately: your agent left the business. 40% of policies are in this position. |
| **Embarrassed** | "I don't even know what I bought or what it pays." | Make not knowing normal. Only 15% completely understand their policy. Never ask them to know their policy number. |
| **Suspicious** | "This is just a way to sell me something." | Say what this is *not*. No cost, no obligation, and the cross-sell questions are switched off entirely when they arrive on their own. |
| **Worried for their family** | "If something happened, would my wife even know who to call?" | Ask the beneficiary question, offer the Policy Location Record. |
| **Stuck** | "Who do I even ring? The last number I had doesn't work." | One button. A named human. A stated response time. |
| **Slightly resentful** | "Nobody has called me in six years." | Acknowledge it plainly and don't defend the industry. An apology costs nothing and it is owed. |

The tone that follows from this: **calm, plain, unhurried, faintly apologetic, never
alarmed.** The competition in this category shouts about protecting your family's future.
We are the ones who say *nothing is wrong, you didn't do anything, here is the four minutes
it takes to put it right.*

## 8. The two doors, and why the questions differ

The same person needs a different conversation depending on how they arrived.

**Agent-sent** — an agent has their policy number and is asking them to confirm details.
Trust is already borrowed from the agent. Coverage and cross-sell questions are
appropriate, because a relationship exists and the client expects advice.

**Client-direct** — they typed the domain in or followed a link, often after a bad
experience. Trust is zero. Asking "would you like a retirement plan?" in the first four
minutes confirms exactly what they suspected. So on this path the needs questions are
replaced by a single open one: *what would you like help with?*

This is the reason the product asks how the visitor got here before it asks anything else,
and it is the reason the agent gets to choose. Selling to a stranger who came looking for
help is the fastest way to lose them; not offering advice to a client whose agent invited
them to a review is a wasted review.

## Method and honesty notes

- Every figure above was taken from a named source and, where possible, confirmed against a
  second independent report. The J.D. Power numbers were confirmed across three outlets.
- The 40% orphan figure traces to ThinkAdvisor and is quoted second-hand by Sureify and
  Coverager; both direct sources returned 403 to automated retrieval, so it is cited as
  reported rather than as read at source. It is the weakest link in the chain and the site
  attributes it rather than stating it as fact.
- Most of the consumer data is **United States**. It is used to describe a pattern, not to
  claim a Trinidad & Tobago statistic. The site says so. The only local figures used are
  from the Insurance Act 2018 and the Central Bank.
- No figure on the site is presented without attribution, and no statistic is used to imply
  a policy is at risk when it isn't.
