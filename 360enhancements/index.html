You said it. It changed.

Nine things, and where each came from.

Nothing here was somebody’s idea of a good system. Each row started as a complaint.

“The review is a black hole. It goes up and I hear nothing.”
→
The review has a clock on itOne business day from the client’s Yes to a decision, and your direct manager is chased if it slips.
“I sent it back weeks ago and nobody has touched it.”
→
A send-back can no longer go quietIt used to fall off every list — the case was no longer waiting on anyone. Eleven were sitting there, the oldest 40 days. Swept daily now: advisor, then DM, then branch manager.
“The letter is the part a client actually keeps.”
→
The letters carry the branchBranch letterhead with the shield, the Insurance Act quoted in full rather than paraphrased, and your name at the top.
“Why does it come from a system address? They reply and I never see it.”
→
Every client email is yoursSent in your name, replies come back to you. Your DM is copied, the branch manager is blind-copied, Sales Support is never copied.
“Approvals were moving before the client had even seen the plan.”
→
Nothing moves until the client says yesThey read it, confirm it, rate the advice and sign online. Only then does the manager’s Agree unlock — on the email, the dashboard and the review page alike. Only the branch manager can open that door, with a reason, written on the case.
“Printing a fact find for a wet signature in 2026?”
→
They sign on their phoneThe signature lands in the client’s box on the fact find. No printing. No scanning.
“They always ask where the policy is and I never have an answer.”
→
Four taps answer for youSigned and submitted · At the branch · Delivered and paid · Not proceeding. The client hears within the minute.
“Nobody chases the file, they chase me in front of the client.”
→
The timers chase us, not the clientNothing keyed at 7 days, undelivered at 10, branch manager at 15. None of it reaches a client.
“I never find out how the delivery actually went.”
→
You are told, two days laterFive questions, answered on a phone in under a minute. Three or under goes to the branch manager alone — never to the wall, never against your name.

One tap, when it happens

Four taps do the telling.

They are on your approval email, on every chase, and on the page you land on after each tap.

Signed and submitted
“Your application is in.”Head office has it. Nothing more for the client to do.
At the branch
“Your policy is here.”Expect your advisor’s call. Starts the 20-day delivery clock.
Delivered and paid
“Your cover is in place.”Counts as API. The survey follows two days later.
Not proceeding
One polite closing note.Out of the pipeline. The door left open.

The timers

They chase us. Not the client.

Day 3
Client has not answered the draft — a gentle reminder In your name, as always.
Day 8
Still nothing — the client is reminded, and you are asked to call A phone call closes what a third email will not.
Day 7
Approved, nothing keyed — the advisor is chased Seven days after the approval, not the submission. DM copied.
Day 10
At the branch, not delivered — the advisor is chased The policy is sitting in the office. The clock is on us.
Day 15
Still not delivered — it reaches the branch manager Escalation. Not a reprimand — a fact somebody has to see.

Branch standard: delivered inside 20 days of the policy reaching the branch.

Who does what now

Four cards, one client.

The system sends every client email and chases every stall. These are the few things each of us still does by hand.

🤵
Card 1The advisor
You do
  • Tap Submit with the client’s email on the fact find. The draft goes in your name.
  • Day 8, no answer: call the client when the email asks you to.
  • Four taps, as they happen — the moment it happens, not at the end of the week.
  • Deliver inside 20 days of the policy reaching the branch.
  • Book the yearly review when the client’s answer lands in your inbox.
The system does
  • The draft, the reminders, the milestones, the survey, the yearly review. All in your name, replies to you.
Never
  • Ask a client to print or wet-sign the fact find.
🔍
Card 2The direct manager
You do
  • Decide within one business day of the client’s Yes.
  • Read the copies. You are copied on every email your advisors’ clients get.
  • Act on the chases: nothing keyed at 7 days, undelivered at 10.
  • Anything you send back, follow. It is swept daily now, but the correction is still a conversation.
The system does
  • Holds Agree until the client has confirmed and signed. Tells you the moment they do.
Never
  • Approve before the client’s Yes. Only the branch manager’s link opens that door, and it is recorded.
🗃
Card 3Sales support
You do
  • Key the application the day it comes in, and tell the advisor to tap Signed and submitted.
  • When a policy arrives, tell the advisor the same day — their tap starts the delivery clock.
  • Fill in the dates. Dispatch, receipt, delivery. A blank date is a client who never hears from us.
The system does
  • Everything the client hears. Nothing goes from Sales Support’s address.
Never
  • Email a client about their plan. You are not copied, by design.
🛡️
Card 4The branch manager
You do
  • Read every low score personally — advice ratings and delivery surveys of 3 or under. They come to you alone.
  • Escalations: a policy 15 days at the branch undelivered lands with you.
  • The override, rarely: approve before a client’s Yes, from your own link, with a reason.
The system does
  • Blind-copies you on every client email. Stamps every send. Never sends twice.
Never
  • Show a low rating against an advisor’s name on the wall.
Thank you. Genuinely.

None of this came from a system. It came from advisors saying the same thing often enough that it became impossible to ignore. Keep telling us what is not working — that is how the next nine get built.
