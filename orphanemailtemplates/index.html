Three letters, because one does not fit an orphan book. Which one a client gets depends on whether they actually hold their policy document — Stage 3 of the process.

These are complete, sendable e-mails. 600px table layout, inline styles, and the masthead points at the hosted PNG on this site — Gmail strips an SVG logo and blocks a data: URI, so any other route arrives with an empty header.

1 · They have their document

Contract delivered and receipted

The standard letter. Nothing is wrong with their file — we are changing who looks after them and want their view before we choose.

From Ricky Rampersad Branch
Subject A change in who looks after your policy
Ricky Rampersad Branch
Guardian Life of the Caribbean

Dear {{FirstName}},

I am writing to let you know that {{AgentName}} is no longer with the company.

Your policy is unaffected. Your contract is with Guardian Life of the Caribbean, not with any individual, so your cover, your premiums and your claim rights all continue exactly as before.

Before we assign anyone new to look after you, we would like to hear from you. We would rather match you to the right person than simply pass your file along.

It takes about four minutes, and there is nothing to prepare.

Tell us how you've been looked after →

For your reference, our records show your premiums paid to {{PaidToDate}}. If that does not match your own records, the form is the right place to tell us — please do rather than assume we already know.

If you would prefer to speak to someone instead, reply to this e-mail or call the branch and we will arrange it.

Ricky Rampersad
Branch Manager, Ricky Rampersad Branch
Guardian Life of the Caribbean Limited
One decision sits inside {{AgentName}}. The recommendation is to name the agent: a client told only that “your agent has left” will work out who they last dealt with and assume worse than the truth. If we are advised otherwise, replace the token with “the agent who looked after your policy” and the sentence still reads.

2 · Document unaccounted for

Released to the agent · no receipt on file

Send only after someone has physically searched for these documents. This version is for the ones not recovered — if a document is found, that client gets letter 3 instead.

From Ricky Rampersad Branch
Subject Your policy document — and a change in who looks after you
Ricky Rampersad Branch
Guardian Life of the Caribbean

Dear {{FirstName}},

Two things, and the second is why I am writing personally.

First, {{AgentName}} is no longer with the company. Your policy is unaffected — your contract is with Guardian Life of the Caribbean, not with an individual.

Second: our records do not show that your policy document was ever handed to you. It was released from this branch on {{ReleasedDate}} and we hold no signed receipt for it. That is our record-keeping, not yours, and I would rather ask you than assume.

Do you have your policy document? If you do, tell us and we will close the gap in our file. If you do not, we will put it right.

Tell us, and choose how to receive it →

You can collect it at the branch, have it couriered, have a new agent bring it in person, or receive it by e-mail. The form asks which you would prefer and when suits you.

Your premiums are paid to {{PaidToDate}}. Nothing about the document affects your cover — you have been insured throughout.

I am sorry this was not done properly the first time.

Ricky Rampersad
Branch Manager, Ricky Rampersad Branch
Guardian Life of the Caribbean Limited
It asks; it does not accuse. A missing receipt means no proof of delivery on file — not proof that nothing was delivered. Some of these clients will have their document and the paperwork was simply never returned. The wording holds either way.

3 · We are holding the document

Never released from the branch

These never left the building, so we can say so plainly. They also tend to be long unpaid, which the letter has to acknowledge without turning into a demand.

From Ricky Rampersad Branch
Subject We are holding your policy document
Ricky Rampersad Branch
Guardian Life of the Caribbean

Dear {{FirstName}},

Your policy document is here at the branch. It was never sent out to you, and that is our error, not yours.

I should also tell you that {{AgentName}} is no longer with the company. Your contract is with Guardian Life of the Caribbean and is unaffected by that.

How would you like to receive it? Collect it at the branch, have it couriered, have someone bring it to you, or receive it by e-mail — whichever suits you.

Choose how to receive your document →

Our records show premiums paid to {{PaidToDate}}, and nothing received since. I am not writing to chase that, and I would like to understand what happened before anyone does.

If the policy stopped because you never received anything, say so on the form and we will take it from there. In many cases a policy in this position can be reinstated.

Ricky Rampersad
Branch Manager, Ricky Rampersad Branch
Guardian Life of the Caribbean Limited

Before any of these send

  • The arrears must be reconciled against cash. All three state a paid-to date in writing. If premium was paid and not applied, we are putting a wrong figure on branch letterhead — and letter 3 leads with it.
  • The unreceipted documents must be physically searched for, and that group split into recovered (→ letter 3) and not recovered (→ letter 2).
  • Offer only what the branch can honour every time. If courier is not certain, take it out of letters 2 and 3. A second broken promise costs more than a narrower first one.
  • One send per mailbox. Where two clients share an inbox, name both rather than sending twice.
  • Some clients have no e-mail. They get a call, not a link — and one needs tracing before anything can reach them.
  • Test-send all three to yourself on a phone before any batch. The gold button on navy is the element most likely to render oddly in an older Outlook.
The page asks; the e-mail tells. A client's paid-to date appears in the letter, which goes to their own address of record. It never appears on the review page, which is public and unauthenticated — a forwarded link must never expose somebody's policy position.
