The Meeting Framework

Ricky Rampersad Branch · what each role does before, during and after a meeting — and until the actions close
Watch the walkthrough → The meeting template → ← The meeting app

Everybody who comes to a branch meeting holds a role, and every role has something to do at each of four stages. The stages are the meeting's own life in the app: a meeting is built, it runs, it is recorded, and its actions are chased until they close. Nothing below is new policy invented for the sake of a diagram — each stage answers something the branch's own meeting record from March to September 2026 shows going wrong, and the finding is printed beside it.

Show me

1 Before

From the chair opening the meeting in the app to the room sitting down.
Why this stage exists The branch meeting ran 113 minutes on 7 August, 82 on 21 August and 54 on 11 September against its own stated target of 45. A meeting assembled in the room takes the room's time to assemble. Everything here happens before anybody sits down.
Chair
  1. Build the meeting in the app — date, start, and the minutes allowed for each item. The agenda prints a clock against every item from those minutes.
  2. Put the carried-forward actions on first. Not new business. Last quarter's open items are what the room has not finished.
  3. Name a presenter against every item. An item with nobody's name on it is yours by default.
  4. Set the materials-due date on the meeting, and set who may see each item: everyone, staff, or chair only.
  5. Publish it — move it from draft to scheduled. Until you do, only staff can see it exists.
Presenter
  1. Your item appears on your Prepare screen the moment the chair assigns it. You do not need to be told.
  2. Upload what you are presenting before the materials-due date. An item marked materials required is not ready until a file is on it.
  3. Mark it Ready. The chair sees what is ready and what is not, so nobody has to chase you and you are never chased for something already in.
  4. Say in one line what the item is for — a decision, a report, or a training point. The room should know what it is being asked for.
Sales support & admin
  1. One number per report, as at the Friday before. Not a figure read live off a screen in the middle of the meeting.
  2. Review each presenter's material and mark it reviewed, so the chair knows it has been looked at by somebody other than its author.
  3. Check the roster. Everyone active needs an access code; without one they cannot sign in, and they will be marked absent for a reason that is not theirs.
Agent
  1. Read the agenda before you come. It is on your phone from the moment it is published.
  2. If you cannot come, log it — sign in and pick the reason. Do it as soon as you know, not on the morning.
  3. Bring your own numbers for anything standing against your name.
Guest
  1. You are added to one meeting, not to the branch. You see that meeting and nothing else.
  2. You see the agenda and whatever is shared with the room. Branch reports stay with staff.
  3. Send anything you are presenting to the chair and it goes on the agenda under your name.

2 During

Check-in opens 30 minutes before the start and closes 90 minutes after it. Signing in after the start is recorded as late.
Why this stage exists Attendance was raised at four meetings running and not one of them has a list the minutes stand behind — 30 July recommends a sign-in sheet, 21 August could not confirm a list from the audio, 11 September records a significant decrease and sends the roll to a form the minutes do not carry. The sign-in is the register now, so the question cannot come up again.
Chair
  1. Start the session in the app. The room follows the current item on their own screens.
  2. Keep the clock. Each item has its minutes; the app shows them running.
  3. Every action gets an owner and a date before you move on. Not "we will look at it".
  4. Close the session when you finish. That stamps the register and writes the absences — it is the one press that closes the roll.
Presenter
  1. Present off the wall, not off a transcript. Figures read from a screen the branch built do not need to be verified afterwards.
  2. Your item is time-boxed — the clock on the agenda is the time you have.
  3. Log the decision your item produced, not the discussion that got there.
Sales support & admin
  1. Minute it as it goes, in the app, under the branch's own sections.
  2. Record every correction to the register — who you marked present by hand, and why. A correction keeps your name on it.
  3. Capture each action with an owner and a date as it is agreed, not from memory afterwards.
Agent
  1. Sign in. That is the register. If you do not sign in you are marked absent — there is nothing else to sign.
  2. Make a contribution — a point, a question, an answer, a concern or a commitment. It is logged against your name and it is what "attended" means.
  3. Phones away. Asked for on 21 August, and by the President on 30 July, who said disengagement in a meeting reads as disrespect to whoever is presenting.
Guest
  1. Sign in like everybody else. You are counted present when you do, and never counted absent when you do not — you are not on the branch roll.
  2. Contribute like anybody in the room. Your points are logged under your name.

3 After

The record, published within two working days of the meeting.
Why this stage exists Each of the last four sets of minutes opens by disclaiming itself — the audio was unclear, the figures should be verified. And one meeting has been filed up to five times across two folders and two accounts, so nobody can say which copy counts. One meeting, one record, one place.
Chair
  1. Read the draft and publish it. Until you publish, agents see no minutes at all.
  2. Check every action has an owner and a date before you publish. An action without both is not an action.
Presenter
  1. Confirm your item was minuted the way you meant it. You are the one who knows.
  2. Your material stays attached to the item — nobody has to go looking for the deck you showed.
Sales support & admin
  1. Publish within two working days. Minutes that arrive the week after are read by nobody.
  2. Minute the decision, not the figure. Figures belong on the wall, where they are live and do not need a verification note under them.
  3. File nothing a second time. The app is the record — there is no second copy to keep in step.
Agent
  1. Read the minutes — they are on your phone as soon as they are published.
  2. Check the actions that name you. You see yours and the ones owned by all agents.
Guest
  1. You see the minutes of the meeting you attended, once the chair publishes them, and only the parts shared with the room.

4 Follow-up

From the minutes going out to the action being closed. This is the stage the branch has never had.
Why this stage exists Across the last five meetings 46 action items were raised and one was marked complete — and that one was done inside the meeting it was raised in. Two of the five sets of minutes carry no status column at all, so nothing could be closed even if it had been. This is a tracking failure, not an effort failure, and it is the cheapest thing on this page to fix.
Chair
  1. Carry every open action forward to the next meeting, as its first item. The app carries them for you.
  2. An action with no movement across two meetings is done, dead or blocked — make it say which. Nothing stays "Open" by default.
  3. Watch the standing threads. Five of them were on the agenda every time the branch met from July to September: scripts and clawback, 75-day premium responses, licensing and CPD, contracts past the 10-day handling period, and fact find before recommendation.
Presenter
  1. An action your item created is yours until it closes, whether or not you present again.
  2. Come back to the next meeting with what happened, not with the plan to make it happen.
Sales support & admin
  1. Every set of minutes carries a status column. Not Started, Open, In Progress, Overdue, Standing, Complete.
  2. Bring a closed list to each meeting — what was finished since the last one, with the date each closed.
  3. Flag anything overdue before the meeting, so the room spends its time on why rather than on discovering it.
Agent
  1. Your actions are on your own screen. You do not have to read the whole tracker to find them.
  2. Move your own item to In Progress or Complete. Nobody can do that for you.
  3. If you are blocked, say so on the item rather than letting it sit. A blocked action somebody knows about is not a late one.
Guest
  1. An action against your name follows you like anyone else's, and the chair will carry it forward until it closes.

Where the findings come from. The branch's own meeting record in Drive — 28 files covering 13 meeting dates, 16 March to 11 September 2026 — read alongside the meeting app's own settings. Check-in windows, late thresholds, action statuses and the agenda sections above are the values the app runs on, not suggestions. The two-working-day publishing target and the carried-forward-first rule are this framework's proposals, and they are the chair's to set.

Stage colours were validated as a set for a dark surface rather than chosen by eye: all four sit inside the OKLCH lightness band, the closest adjacent pair separates by ΔE 9.6 for a protan reader, and every one clears 3:1 against the page. No stage is identified by colour alone — each is numbered and named, so this page reads in greyscale and in print.
