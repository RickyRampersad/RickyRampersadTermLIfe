How to prepare for next week

Everything below is drawn from an item that is still open in the record. Bring the answer, not the plan to answer. Tap a role to show only that list.

Every agent
  1. Log in to the meeting page. Your login is the attendance register now — there is no separate sheet to sign.
  2. Your 75-day responses, submitted. Your portal pipeline stays halted until they are in, and the branch total went up, not down, the last time it was counted.
  3. Your outstanding scripts, cleared or queried. If one is not on the report, confirm with Sales Support before escalating — it usually means AS400 has not been updated.
  4. Contracts out of your hands. 40 were sitting with agents on 11 September. Bring the count you still hold and the date each goes out.
  5. Your licence renewal documents in, if yours falls between now and December.
  6. Your own fact-find count and quality score, against the branch benchmark. The branch score was 68% against a target of 100%.
Unit managers
  1. Your 75-day manager responses. Thirteen were outstanding across managers on 7 August. The client contact is mandatory even where the client has already paid.
  2. Your fact-find approval queue, cleared. After three days the client is told automatically that it is still waiting on you.
  3. Your red-zone five-year persistency agents — with what has been done since August, not what is planned. One agent disputed their banding on 21 August and the recheck is still open.
  4. Your unit’s lapse count for July, August and September. No meeting since July has reported the monthly number.
Sales support & admin
  1. One number per report, as at the Friday before. Not a figure read live off a screen mid-meeting.
  2. A closed column. Which items from 11 September are done, with the date each closed. Two of the last five meetings had no status column at all.
  3. The Salesforce contract-received refresh — open since 11 September, and the reason a contract can read outstanding when it is not.
  4. The fact-find signing and PDF routing fault, raised on 11 September and still unresolved.
  5. MPOS devices — how many are still out, by name.
The chair
  1. Open on closing FY2026, not on new business. Eight threads crossed the year end; the room has not met since they did.
  2. One owner and one date on every action, and a status column on every set of minutes from here. 46 raised and 1 closed is a tracking failure, not an effort failure.
  3. Say what the 25% quota reduction means now the year has turned — whether it carries into FY2027 or lapses with FY2026.
  4. Set the cadence in the room. Two gaps this year, nine weeks and five weeks, and the week numbering hides them: W20 to W22 is labelled two weeks and is twelve.
