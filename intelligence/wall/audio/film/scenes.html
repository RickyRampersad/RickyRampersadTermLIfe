The Branch
Intelligence Wall.

Four screens. Live on the branch floor, twenty four hours a day.

Insurance Act 2018 · s266 · Schedule 11
“Registrants and insurance consultants shall comply with the standards on market conduct as prescribed in Schedule 11.”

A registrant is any person registered as an insurer or an intermediary. And s2 defines an intermediary as “an agent, agency, broker, brokerage, sales representative and adjuster”. So the standard is not the company’s to carry — it is yours, personally. That is what every screen on this wall is measuring.

1 Premium dues · the 45-day line

A premium that stopped coming in. Forty five days. Sixty. Ninety. Who crossed the line today, how long they have held cover, how they pay, and whose they are.

Look closer

At forty five the grace period has passed — and a phone call still works. This is today’s list, not the month’s.

Look closer

How long the client has held the policy. A thirty year client is not a forty five day letter — and the wall writes a different one for each band.

1 Premium dues · at sixty days

We write again — and the letter remembers what they told us the first time.

Insurance Act 2018 · s180 & s177
“An insurer shall not forfeit a policy for non-payment … until a notice has been sent … and twenty business days have elapsed.”

And after three years of premiums, s177 makes the value already the client’s. A policy lost through silence is not a lapse — it is a duty nobody discharged.

2 Contracts we hold · whose hands is it in

Two problems, and they are not the same problem. One is ours. One is the agent’s. This wall keeps them apart.

Still in our cabinet

The contract arrived from head office and no agent has collected it. Nobody outside this building can fix that. That delay is ours.

With an agent, unsigned

An agent collected it, and the client has still not signed for it. The contract is out of our hands and not yet in theirs. That delay is the agent’s.

Look closer

Every row is a name and a number of days — how many that agent is holding, and how long the oldest one has waited.

3 Delivery · contract delivery 2026

Everything dispatched this year that has not reached the client — measured from the day head office sent it, not from the day we noticed.

Insurance Act 2018 · s268(1)
“In the case of an individual life policy, upon acceptance of the risk, an insurer shall issue a policy within twenty business days of acceptance of the risk.”

That is the company’s clock, and it stops at issue. The Act sets no deadline for the days between our cabinet and the client’s hands.

Our own ten days

So the clock is ours. We set it at ten days, and we show it — against every band, in public, on the floor.

4 Licensing · the licence year

Two licences, not one. Life, and general — two separate registrations, on two separate anniversaries.

The year ahead

Every renewal month for the twelve months ahead, on one screen. This month first, and click any month to hold the wall to it.

Life and general

Life on one line. General on the other. Some agents hold both — and both have to be renewed, separately.

Insurance Act 2018 · s114(6) & s119(1)
“No person shall perform the functions of a sales representative during any period in which he is not registered.”

And s119(1): a licence is not renewed without continuing professional development. A lapsed licence is not paperwork. It is business nobody in this branch is allowed to write.

On every wall

It names the agent.
The unit. The manager.

Nobody on this wall is only being watched. Management is measured on the same screen, in the same room, at the same time as everybody else.
