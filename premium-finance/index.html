Questions people ask
Straight answers, before you apply.
Why is a shorter plan cheaper?

Interest is charged each month only on what you still owe. A shorter plan pays the balance down faster, so there is less owing each month and less interest overall. A flat-rate plan charges on the whole premium for the whole term, even after most of it is repaid.

What does 2% a month come to over a year?

2% a month is 24% a year nominal, or about 26.8% a year effective. But you only borrow for 6 to 10 months, and on a falling balance, so what you actually pay is the cost of financing shown on your quote, and nothing else.

Am I covered while I'm still paying?

Yes. Your insurer is paid in full before your first instalment, so the policy is in force from its start date, exactly as if you had paid the premium yourself.

Can I pay it off early?

Yes. Because interest runs on the balance, paying early means you pay interest only up to the day you settle. Ask the officer for a settlement figure.

What do I need to apply?

Your ID, and the insurer's quotation, invoice or renewal notice for the policy. The officer will ask for copies after you submit.

Which policies can be financed?

Motor, property and most general insurance premiums, and some life and health premiums. The officer confirms with your insurer that the policy allows premium financing and that unused premium is refundable on cancellation.
