Branch process pack

The revised templates, scripts and process — read yours, then press OK

Sign in with the same agent number and password you use for the assignment board. You will be shown the sections for your role.

 

 

 

One list. Every address below is the branch's own; none of them is a link a client ever holds.

  • /orphanmanagement — the assignment board. Your clients, their history, and where you log every call.
  • /logbook — the Log Book. Every document in and out.
  • /premium-due — the premium due desk.
  • /groupclientmanagement — group clients and the Monday letter.
  • /meetings — the Wednesday meeting.
  • The workflow page — the whole flow on one page, by role.
What changed: they are one list, here, and you sign in to all of them with the same agent number and password.
  • Never characterise anyone who left. State Guardian Life's own notice and nothing else. Never why, never where they went, never that anyone is saying otherwise.
  • Only a licensed agent advises. Anything about cover, suitability, or whether a client should change a policy is a call back from a licensed agent — whoever is asked.
  • Log it the same day, where the system reads it. A conversation nobody files did not happen, and a note on a private spreadsheet reaches nothing.

If a client repeats something a former agent told them, answer none of it. Give them the number to confirm, and note who, when, how, and what — in the client's own words.

If they say … has the wording for the questions that come up most.

  • Client names, numbers and figures stay in the systems. Not in WhatsApp, not in a personal spreadsheet, not in an e-mail to a personal address.
  • Your password is yours. Nobody needs it — not a colleague, not the branch, not anyone who telephones asking for it.
  • Sign out on a shared device. Signing out clears the client that was open and everything the device had saved.
Why this is here now: the systems hold the whole branch book. One shared password reaches further than it used to.

Sunday to Sunday is the week. It closes itself on Sunday evening, the agenda builds itself on Wednesday morning, and the meeting runs 9:00 to 10:30.

  • Signing in on the app is the register. Nobody is marked in from the front. If you do not sign in you are marked absent; if you cannot come, log it and give a reason.
  • You get a note each working morning with your own cases, what is due, and what you are presenting.
  • The screen is the meeting. Only the exceptions are spoken aloud.
What changed: the week runs Sunday to Sunday so Wednesday reports a finished week, not half of one.

Each working morning you get one e-mail: the cases on your list this week, what is overdue, where your goals stand, and what you are presenting on Wednesday.

It is short on purpose. If it says nothing, you owe nothing — it is not sent when there is nothing to say.

New. It starts with a small group before the branch gets it. If a figure on it is wrong, reply and say so.

Open the client on the board, press Log call, choose what happened, and write the note. That one press writes your file note, stamps the record, and posts to the client's Salesforce task.

  • A no answer is a try, not a close. Mark it as a try.
  • A task closes only with a note of who you spoke to and what was agreed.
  • It stays open until the agent is named and the conversation has happened.
What changed: work logged only in Salesforce Chatter does not reach the branch's own counts. Log it on the board and it reaches both.

Every Wednesday a few agents account for their own week, by name, for two minutes. The rule is fixed and the screen picks, not the chair:

  • Anyone the Monday list sent cases to who answered none of them comes up first, most cases first.
  • The remaining slots go to whoever has gone longest without one.

So: answer your cases and the screen never calls you. Have a clean week and you will still come up eventually, with something that worked.

New. “Could not reach them” is a complete answer. Never replying is not.

Every document you hand in is logged with your agent number: the document is chosen from a list, the department is routed for you, and staff sign for it.

  • A contract you collect to deliver is yours for 20 days. You are chased at 15 and 18, and the manager is told at 20.
  • Some documents travel together — the page asks before you log one without its companion.
  • You see your own clients only.

Your goals are filed once and then stream on the wall: contacts, appointments, fact finds, applications and API, as a week's commitment.

No filed plan means no fact find. The branch reads filed plans against the week actually worked, as a shortfall in cases rather than as a percentage — eleven of the thirty-one fact finds the plans ask for is a sentence somebody can act on.

Your call list is a tab on the board. Open the client, press Log call, record what happened, and flag a call-back where one is needed.

What changed, and it matters: the old Client Support calls spreadsheet is one-way. Nothing typed on it ever reaches the board, the reports or the chase. On the first day of calls nine e-mails were typed into the sheet and none was recorded; by the next afternoon it was 107. Record it on the board or it reaches nothing.

Your scripts: your day, step by step · the call, word for word · if they say …

Read every e-mail address back to the client and tick that you read it back. The page refuses an address that looks cut off or misspelt, but it cannot catch one that is simply wrong.

  • A new number or address only if it has changed.
  • An address a letter bounced from is never filed back, even if the client confirms it.
  • Your own name goes on every detail you take, so nothing has to be retyped.

You ask questions, take details and explain process. You never advise on cover, on whether a policy suits, or on whether a client should change one.

Anything of that kind is a call back from a licensed agent, and saying so is a complete and professional answer. The objective of the call is clean data and a correct record, so the right agent can be named.

During the week you check a group's open items, give a reason for anything open more than 30 days, preview the letter, tick that you checked, and press Approve for Monday.

  • The letters send themselves at 10:00 on Monday. You approve; you do not send.
  • A letter waits, with the reason, if an item arrived that you never saw. The letter goes only with what a person checked.
  • Every staff member sees every group. Only the owner, the deputy and the branch manager change one.
  • A group's answer marked Needs a change or Not ours stays on “To act on” for fourteen days.

The group manual and test.

  • Sign for every document the same working day. Correct the routing when the suggestion is wrong — that is what teaches it.
  • Build the transmittal the day you receive the document. Where that is not possible, record the reason before the hourly run asks you for it.
  • Answer the department's day-3 confirmation. Where a department says not received, respond with everyone copied and open the Salesforce task.
Measured: 41% of documents went out the day they were logged and 12% took six days or more. Same or next working day is the standard.

Every portal enrolment e-mails you the details. The pack is yours to finish:

  • Chase the signed printed form, the ID copy, and for health the bank details.
  • Flip Signed form received to Yes.
  • Send the pack to Guardian.

Work the pending requirements list daily. It is the branch's largest single source of lost business, and it is the one list where a day's delay costs a case.

Pensions: Salesforce cannot tell a company scheme from a personal annuity — one record type covers both and ownership is recorded on 8 records in 402. Until that is filled in, any question about a company scheme ends with a person, and that person is you. Record Ownership whenever you touch a pension record.

Every Wednesday you have a slot of your own with your unit's numbers already on the screen: cases answered against sent, who answered nothing, your register, your overdue actions, and how many of your people spoke at all last week.

You are not reading the figures out. The room can see them. You are saying what you are doing about them.

New, and it is the point of the change. The slot is seeded from the roster and cannot be left blank. It does not fall to the chair if you are away — it stays yours and the meeting says so.

You see your team's clients, their figures, what each agent has logged, and how long each named client has waited. Nothing logged two working days after a naming is flagged, and that flag is yours to clear.

An agent who reports only in Salesforce Chatter shows as nothing logged until they mark the call or write the feedback field. Tell them.

  • You do not name agents. The branch manager and Client Support do.
  • You do not e-mail a client a note from the board. Those go in the branch manager's name.
  • You see your unit, not the branch. Another unit's figures are not yours, and neither are the branch-wide exception lists.

Several systems are built and deliberately silent. Each needs you:

  • Automatic client e-mail is held since 7 October — letters, reminders, receipts and introductions.
  • The TT$200 claims are found and recorded, and sent nowhere.
  • The premium due letters need re-approving: the 13 October rewording voided the last approval.
  • The Delivery Book and Log Book default to test mode.
None of these is visible from outside the script editor. Nobody walking past a screen can tell that the claims are held. That is what the register below is for.
  • The go-live switches, and the holds.
  • A note e-mailed to a client from the board — it goes in your name, so your press is your go.
  • The Monday rehearsal, and Send now on a group letter.
  • Confirming a staff member's written test marks.

When the process changes, edit the pack and bump its version. Every acknowledgement against the old version goes stale at once and the register says who is behind — which is the only way the branch can show, on a date, that a named person was handed a named process.
