Why this exists

A renewal is a phone call we either make, or lose.

Nothing about our book is broken. What slips is the follow-up — the call that doesn't get made because nobody could see, on a Monday morning, which twenty clients were up this fortnight. This system makes that list, sends the first contact for you, and shows who has answered.

What the year actually looked like

Retention was 81% in January. By March and April it was 47% and 45%. By August it was back to 79%. The book didn't change in those months. The calling did. That swing is worth about TT$85,000 a year, and it is the entire reason this exists.

Every figure on this page is real, taken from Salesforce — except the client names, which are invented. Nothing here can send an email or change a policy.

The wall

The screen everyone walks past.

The same numbers, on a rotating board for the branch. Nobody has to ask how the month is going — and nobody has to build a report to answer it.

What rotates on it

The month at a glance · every renewal on a date rail · the motor register · the property register · values — motor (last year's insured value against this year's, and the market check) · values — property (how stale each sum insured is) · the year's trend · whom we're losing · who's processing the renewals · the money · open tasks · next month.

Your part

Three habits, and the system runs itself.

None of this works on its own. These are the three things that have to happen in Salesforce for the board to tell the truth — tick them as we go through.

Why the third one matters most

This year we re-wrote TT$322,800 of premium. Payments are recorded against TT$30,700 of it — about 10%. The money is almost certainly collected; it just isn't on the record. Until a receipt is posted, the board can't count it, and neither can head office.

Questions, or something doesn't look right on your list? Tell Ricky — a wrong figure on a wall everyone walks past is worse than no wall, so say so early.
