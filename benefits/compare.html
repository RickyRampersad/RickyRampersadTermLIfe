Retirement structures · Trinidad & Tobago

There is more than one way to give your staff a pension.

Most employers are quoted a trust-based group pension plan and never told there is an alternative. The alternative is not always better — but the difference in running cost is large enough that you should see both before you decide. This page explains the three structures, what the law requires of each, and what they cost you every year.

01 — The three structures

What you are actually choosing between

The first two are occupational pension plans held in trust and registered with the Central Bank. The third is not a pension plan at all in the legal sense — it is a set of individual contracts your company pays into.

Group · Trust

Defined Benefit

The employer promises a pension — typically a formula on final salary and years of service.

  • The employer carries the investment risk. If the fund underperforms, the company funds the shortfall.
  • Actuarial valuation is not optional — it establishes whether the plan can meet its promise.
  • Highest governance load and the least predictable cost.
  • Largely closed to new entrants across the market. Rarely the right answer for a new plan.
Group · Trust

Defined Contribution

The employer promises a contribution, not an outcome. Whatever the fund grows to is the pension.

  • The employee carries the investment risk. Cost to the company is predictable.
  • Still a trust: trustees, registration, triennial actuarial review, audited accounts.
  • Vesting is available — you can make the employer share earn out over time.
  • Institutional pricing on investment management, spread across the whole group.
Individually owned

Corporate-paid individual plans

The company pays premiums into a BIR-approved deferred annuity owned by each employee.

  • No trust, no trustees, no plan registration, no triennial valuation, no plan audit.
  • The employee owns the contract from day one — fully portable, no leaver administration.
  • No vesting. Once a premium is paid it belongs to the employee. You cannot claw it back.
  • Charges sit inside each contract and are typically front-loaded into the early years.

A fourth route exists and is worth naming: a Corporate Tax Incentive Savings Plan arrangement, where bonus and allowance earnings are paid by the employer into the employee's approved plan without first being taxed. It is a variant of the third column rather than a structure of its own.

02 — What the law requires

The position in Trinidad and Tobago

This is where the cost difference comes from. Everything below applies to a trust-based occupational plan — columns one and two. Almost none of it applies to column three.

Who regulates it
Occupational pension plans established under trust are registered and supervised by the Central Bank of Trinidad and Tobago under the Insurance Act, 2018. The Central Bank oversees trustees' compliance with the Act. As at Dec 2021 there were 177 active private occupational pension plans registered.
Trust deed and rules
To qualify for registration the deed must provide for the objects of the plan, the appointment and removal of trustees, the vesting of all plan property in the trustees, investment of capital moneys in the trustees' names, and employer contributions.
Actuarial review
Section 185(2) of the Insurance Act requires an actuarial investigation into the financial condition of the plan, and a report of the findings, every three years — or more often if the Central Bank specifies. This is the single largest recurring professional cost in a small plan.
Tax approval
Separately from Central Bank registration, the plan must be approved by the Board of Inland Revenue to be an Approved Pension Fund Plan. Employer contributions are deductible. Approved deferred annuities must likewise be BIR-approved for the contributions to be deductible.
Section 134
Individually owned approved annuities written under Section 134 are tax-deductible up to the greater of one third of chargeable income or 20% of emolument income, and can be transferred to another approved plan when the employee changes employer. This is the provision the LifeSecure corporate route relies on.
The TT$60,000 ceiling
Published guidance also states that an individual's relief on approved pension fund plans, approved annuity plans and National Insurance is aggregated and capped at TT$60,000. Unresolved: this ceiling and the Section 134 formula above do not obviously reconcile. Before any Canopy quote computes a tax figure, confirm with BIR which limit governs, and whether the $60,000 cap sits over the Section 134 calculation or beside it. Getting this wrong overstates the client's saving.
Fees
There are no legal limits on fees charged by plan or scheme managers and other service providers. Annuity charges are commonly deducted from premiums across the first one to ten years of the contract. Which is exactly why the comparison below has to be done on real numbers, not defaults.
Reform in train
An Occupational Pension Plans Bill has been under policy development, and the Central Bank has published work on national pension reform. Any structural advice should be re-tested against the law in force on the day it is given.

04 — The honest balance

Where individually-owned wins, and where it costs you

Genuine advantages

  • No fixed plan overhead. No triennial actuarial review, no plan audit, no trustees, no Central Bank registration to maintain.
  • Scales down cleanly. Fixed costs spread over few members are brutal; removing them helps a small group most.
  • No leaver administration. The contract belongs to the employee and simply travels with them.
  • Fits contract-based employment. Staff on renewable annual contracts are covered from day one with no eligibility or vesting machinery.
  • Simple to explain. Every employee has their own plan and their own statement.
  • No trustee liability sitting on your directors or managers.

What you give up

  • No vesting, no forfeiture. The retention lever is gone — paid is paid, even if someone leaves in month three. This is the decisive trade-off.
  • The cost is implicit, not absent. LifeSecure charges no setup, ongoing or exit fee and allocates 100% of contributions — but the insurer still earns, through the spread between the fund's return and the rate credited. A nil-fee plan is not a nil-cost plan, and a client who is told otherwise will feel misled later.
  • No employer control over investment strategy, and no single governance point.
  • Individual tax relief is capped and shared with NIS and any personal annuity already held. Higher earners may get no relief on the top slice.
  • Fragmented reporting. No consolidated plan view, no group-level member data, harder to demonstrate the benefit at a staff meeting.
  • Not an occupational pension plan in the legal sense — which matters if a tender, union agreement or collective agreement specifies one.

Say this out loud to every client

"Cheaper to run" and "better for your people" are two different questions. Removing the trust removes the employer's fixed costs, but it moves the member onto retail contract charges. On a large group with strong institutional pricing, the trust often wins on the member's net outcome even though it costs the company more to administer. On a small group, it usually does not.

The number that settles it is the total charge borne by the member over the full term — not the employer's annual admin bill. That comparison is the last table above, and it needs real quoted charges in it, not the placeholders shipped with this page.

05 — What the intake must establish

The deep-dive questions

Structure cannot be chosen from headcount and payroll alone. These are the questions that actually decide it, and they belong in the guided flow before any quote is produced.

QuestionWhy it decides the structure
Is there an existing registered plan, and is it DB or DC?An existing DB plan cannot simply be swapped — it carries accrued liabilities and a wind-up process.
Do you want the employer share to be forfeitable if someone leaves early?A yes rules out individually-owned outright. This is the single most decisive question.
What proportion of staff are on contract, and what is your annual turnover?High turnover makes vesting valuable; low turnover makes it near-worthless and the trust hard to justify.
Are any employees already contributing to a personal annuity or NIS at a high salary?Drives the TT$60,000 aggregate relief cap and who actually gets tax benefit.
Is a pension plan required by a collective agreement, union agreement or client tender?If yes, individually-owned contracts may not satisfy the requirement at all.
Who would act as trustee, and is the board willing to accept trustee duties?No willing trustee means no trust-based plan, whatever the economics say.
What in-house capacity exists to administer enrolment and remittance?Determines whether third-party administration is a cost line or a necessity.
Is the intention to retire staff in-house, or is portability the priority?Portability favours individually-owned; a retirement-age workforce favours the trust.

What this page is. An education and comparison tool. It sets out the structural and regulatory differences between retirement arrangements available to employers in Trinidad and Tobago, and models their cost on assumptions you supply.

What it is not. It is not financial, legal, actuarial or tax advice, and it does not recommend a structure. Choosing between a registered occupational pension plan and individually owned contracts has tax, legal and employment consequences specific to each employer. Confirm the current position with the Board of Inland Revenue and the Central Bank of Trinidad and Tobago, and take licensed advice, before acting.
