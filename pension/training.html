Section 1

The thing you are selling

A company takes out a pension policy on an employee's life, pays the premiums, and deducts every dollar it pays. The company owns the policy. The employee is the person it pays out to, and it is held for them and their estate.

That is Section 134 of the Income Tax Act, Chapter 75:01. In a sentence you can say across a desk: "You can pay to keep the people you cannot afford to lose, and write off what you pay."

Why a company says yes

  • A raise is spent by Friday. A pension is a reason to still be there in ten years.
  • The cost is deductible — it is not the same money as a raise.
  • It is visible. The employee can see it, watch it build, and name who receives it.

Why an employee says yes

  • It costs them nothing. It is on top of salary, not out of it.
  • If they add something of their own, that part lowers the income tax they pay.
  • They name the beneficiaries, and they can change them.
Say this, not that. Never say "the company is giving you a pension" — the company owns the policy. Say: "your employer has taken out a pension on your life, they pay for it, and you are the person it pays out to." That is both accurate and better.
Section 2

The five rules you must know

Get these wrong and the B.I.R. sends it back. Everything else the system works out for you.

Only the company's contribution is added to income

The 6(a) annual premium plus any 6(b) lump sum is added to the employee's emolument income before the limits are worked out. A personal deferred annuity or another approved pension is a deduction only — it is never added. Getting this backwards is the commonest mistake there is.

The maximum is the greater of two tests

One third of chargeable income (Line 9), or 20% of total emolument income (Line 11). The greater. Never the smaller, and never the two added together.

Exactly one of Line 10 and Line 12 may read Yes

They are independent checks. Both reading Yes means the contribution is over the maximum and the B.I.R. will not approve it. The wizard will not let you print a form where both say Yes — it scales back to the maximum and tells you on screen what was entered instead.

N.I.S. is looked up from the base salary

Never from the contribution-inclusive total. The wizard does this correctly; you only need to know it so that you can answer when a client's accountant asks.

The employee's own contribution has a $200 a month minimum

Below that it is not worth writing. It is deductible to them at Line 6(a)(iii).

Section 119. A false declaration is an offence — a fine of $8,000 and up to three years' imprisonment. You never sign for a client, you never guess a figure to fill a gap, and you never tell a client "just put anything there".

The one number to hold in your head

On a salary of $240,000 with no other deductions, the most the company may contribute is $62,660 a year. That is a useful anchor when somebody asks "roughly how much?" — but always run the real figure, because a client's own pension, annuity or allowances move it.

The code is the sign-in. There is no password to invent, forget or reset — which is the whole reason it works for a client who will use it four times a year. Codes avoid the letters O, I and S and the digits 0, 1 and 5, so they survive being read down a telephone. If a client says "is that an oh or a zero" — it is neither, look again.
Section 4

Running a case in the wizard

Five steps. Type the case once and it fills every form.

Step 1 — the Section 134 computation

Company and employee details, the salary, what the company wants to put in, the plan and the start date. The maximum is worked out live, the two limits are drawn so you can show the client which one binds, and the B.I.R. request prints from here.

You do this Check the maximum before you promise anything.

Step 2 — Declaration 1

The employee's own declaration: their details, their contribution, their plan, and their beneficiaries. Beneficiary shares must total exactly 100% and anyone named needs photo ID.

Step 3 — Declaration 2

The third-party declaration, used because the company owns the policy. The company signs and stamps on top; the employee signs underneath. Not the other way round.

Step 4 — K.Y.C.

Photo ID, a utility bill in the client's name under six months old, a recent pay slip, and photo ID for every named beneficiary. For a company proposer: certified formation documents and ID for every director and any shareholder holding 10% or more.

Step 5 — review and submit

Preview the Full Package — one PDF, fifteen pages on a typical case: cover sheet, the B.I.R. request and the computation, both declarations, the salary deduction form and the K.Y.C. images. Download it, then email it to the branch. Also build the employee's own three-page pack and send it to them.

Still to attach by hand. An original or certified pay slip and a copy of the approved TD1 go with the B.I.R. request. The package does not contain them.

If the case was enrolled online

When an employer and employee have both completed their parts on the website, you do not retype any of it. At the top of Step 1 put the employee's enrolment code into "Already enrolled online? Load it" and the whole case fills — 28 fields. Then add the K.Y.C. images and enter the beneficiaries as rows in Step 2 (the loader shows you their exact wording to copy), and print.

It will refuse a half-finished enrolment. If the employee has not done their part yet, nothing loads and it tells you so. A half-filled Declaration is worse than a blank one.
Section 5

Setting a company up on the register

Done once per company, in the branch Sheet. Ten minutes, and then that company can run its own enrolments.

Create the tabs, if they are not there

Guardian Renewals → Pension — create register tabs.

Add the company

On Pension Companies: company name, B.I.R. file number, address, date of incorporation, the contact and their email, the agent and the agent's number. Leave the code column blank.

Address, incorporation date and agent number are here rather than on each employee, so they are typed once and then appear on every Declaration and every B.I.R. form for that company.

Issue the codes

Guardian Renewals → Pension — issue missing codes. Codes already in the sheet are never touched, so a code you have already given a client keeps working for ever.

Send the employer their code

Either the code on its own, or the link that fills it in: pensionplantt.com/?code=CO-XXXX-XXXX. Guardian Renewals → Pension — show the sign-in link has it to hand.

Before any of this works, the web app must be republished once. Apps Script → Deploy → Manage deployments → edit → New version. Until that is done, sign-in says so politely and offers the branch email. Ask whoever looks after the Sheet if you are not sure.
Section 6

The online enrolment

This is the part that runs without you. Know it well enough to explain it, because a client who understands it will not ring you to ask what is happening.

The employer completes their part

Employer Signed in, they press Enrol an employee: the employee's name and email, position, department, employed since, the plan, the premium, any lump sum, salary, commencement, maturity age, guarantee, and the first payroll deduction date.

The employee is written to that minute

Automatic They are congratulated, told plainly what a company-owned plan is, told what the company is putting in for them, and asked for their part. Their access code is in the email. The employer, the branch and the team are all copied.

They are reminded every morning at 9

Automatic Until their part is in. Each reminder is a reply on the same email thread, so the whole history sits underneath it, and carries a list of everything sent so far.

After 45 days the emails stop by themselves, the case is marked "Stalled — needs a phone call", and it comes to us. Past that point a daily email is noise. Ring them.

The employee completes their part

Employee Date of birth and home address (both required — the declarations cannot be completed without them), B.I.R. file number, marital status, anything they add of their own, their retirement age and guarantee, who they name, and photographs of three documents.

Both sides are thanked

Automatic The enrolment closes and both are told it is going to the B.I.R. for approval of the company's plan and the employee's own, with a link for questions.

A progress note every ten days

Automatic To both sides, on the same thread, until the policy is delivered — and one more on the day it is.

The five steps everyone watches

The employer's part → the employee's part → B.I.R. approval → policy issued → policy delivered. The employer sees a card per employee with the timeline and who is holding it up; the employee sees the same five for their own plan. It is the same definition in the website, the emails and the Sheet, so nobody is ever told three different things.

Section 7

Moving a case along

Three of the five steps are ours to mark. Do it the day it happens, because both sides see it and the next progress note carries it.

Open the enrolment's row

On the Pension Enrollments tab, put the cursor anywhere on that employee's row.

Mark the next step

Guardian Renewals → Pension — mark the next step done. It tells you which step is next and asks you to confirm. At policy issued it asks for the policy number.

At delivery it writes to both sides immediately

You do not have to send anything.

You cannot mark the employee's part done. That step is theirs to finish, and the system will tell you so. If they genuinely cannot use the website, take their details on the telephone and enter the case in the wizard instead — then it never becomes an online enrolment at all.
Section 8

Answering the hard questions

The ones you will actually be asked, and answers you can give without checking.

"What happens to it if I leave?"
The company owns the policy, so what happens depends on what the company decides and on the plan's terms. Do not guess — say "that is decided by your employer and the policy terms, and I will get you the exact answer in writing", and then do.
"How much will it be worth when I retire?"
Say what is guaranteed and what is not. Never quote a projected fund value across a desk — the branch illustrates those properly. Charges in the contracts are "current" figures the Company may alter, so they are the basis for advice, never a quotation.
"Can we put in more for someone?"
There is a ceiling and it is not a flat percentage — run it in Step 1 and show them the two limits. The answer is often "yes, more than you think", and the screen proves it.
"Why does the company sign on top?"
Because the company is the proposer and owns the policy. Declaration 2 is the third-party declaration. The employee signs underneath as the life assured.
"Is my information safe?"
Their code shows their record and nobody else's. The employer cannot see their code. Every sign-in is logged. The wizard holds a case in the agent's own browser and nowhere else.
"Can I change who I named?"
Yes. Tell us and we handle it. Encourage them to review it after a marriage, a birth or a death.
RIA is on every page — the gold button, bottom right. It knows the case on screen and the plan terms from the actual contracts. If you are unsure in front of a client, open it. It will also tell them honestly when something needs the branch.

When something goes wrong

What you seeWhat it meansWhat to do
"That code was not recognised"Wrong code, or the client has mistyped it. Check it against the Sheet. Remember there are no O, I, S, 0, 1 or 5 in a code.
"The branch may need to republish it"The web app is running an older deployment. Apps Script → Deploy → Manage deployments → New version.
"The register could not be reached"No signal, or the Sheet is unreachable. Try again. The wizard, the computation and every PDF work with no network at all.
Both Line 10 and Line 12 say YesThe contribution is over the maximum. The printed form is scaled back to the maximum automatically. Tell the client what changed and why.
Beneficiary shares are not 100%They will not add up. The wizard flags it. Fix it before printing — Head Office will send it back otherwise.
An enrolment is "Stalled"45 days with no response from the employee. Telephone them. The daily emails have stopped on purpose.
Never fill the address, identification or expiry fields on page 3 of either Declaration. They belong to the "declaration in case where the proposer is illiterate" block and identify the attesting witness in that specific case. Filling them on an ordinary application asserts something about your client that is not true.
Section 10

The one-page checklist

Print this one and keep it in the folder.

Before you promise anything

  • Run the maximum in Step 1 with the real salary and the client's real deductions.
  • Check which limit binds, and say so out loud.

Before you print

  • Beneficiary shares total exactly 100%.
  • Photo ID for the client and every named beneficiary.
  • Utility bill in the client's name, under six months old.
  • Recent pay slip — and the original or certified copy for the B.I.R.
  • A copy of the approved TD1.
  • For a company proposer: certified formation documents, ID for every director and any 10%+ shareholder.
  • Page 3 illiterate-declarant fields left blank on both Declarations.

Before you send

  • Preview the Full Package and look at every page.
  • Company stamps and signs Declaration 2 on top; employee signs underneath.
  • Email the package to support@rickyrampersadbranch.com.
  • Send the employee their own three-page pack.

After it is in

  • Mark B.I.R. approved, policy issued (with the number) and delivered the day each happens.
  • Both sides are told automatically. You do not send progress emails by hand.
