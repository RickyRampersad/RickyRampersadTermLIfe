The problem

You cannot afford
to lose your best people.

A raise is spent by Friday. A pension is a reason to still be here in ten years — and under Section 134 of the Income Tax Act, the company that pays for it deducts every dollar.

What it is

Three facts, and that is the whole structure.

The owner

The company owns it

The policy is the company's asset. It is taken out on the employee's life, and the company holds it.

The cost

The company deducts it

Every premium the company pays is a deductible expense — that is what Section 134(6) is for.

The benefit

The employee is paid

They are the annuitant. It is held for them and their estate — which is precisely why it holds them.

The employee may add to it out of salary as well. That part is theirs, and it lowers the income tax they pay.

Line 6 — of the B.I.R. request

There is a ceiling, and it is not a flat percentage.

The contribution itself counts as part of the employee's income before the limit is worked out. The two move together — which is why the figure has to be solved, not multiplied out.

6 (a)

The annual premium

What the company pays in every year.

6 (b)

Any lump sum

A one-off payment on top, in the same year.

Lines 9 & 11 — the two tests

Two tests. You may use the greater.
